# Matthew 23:19 — 4K Verse Analysis

**Part of**: Matthew 23:13-36 (Seven Woes: Woes Against the Scribes and Pharisees)
**Original Language**: Greek (NA28/Nestle 1904)
**Analyst**: Peaches (4K Bible Translation Harness)

---

## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> You blind men! For which is greater, the gift or the altar that makes the gift sacred?

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

You blind men! For which is greater — the gift, or the altar that sanctifies the gift? (The Greek uses a vocative plural adjective "blind" as a direct address to men who cannot see, and the participle "sanctifying" carries the active, ongoing sense of making holy — not merely "making sacred" as a static state, but an ongoing process of conferring holiness upon the offering.)

---

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

| # | Standard (ESV) | What's Lost | Why It Matters |
|---|----------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | "makes the gift sacred" | The ESV flattens "sanctifying" (a present active participle) into a static result. The Greek participle conveys an ongoing process — the altar actively sanctifies, not merely has made sacred. | Sanctification is not a one-time event but a continuous relationship between altar and offering. The altar's holiness is actively conferred, not merely inherited. |
| 2 | "the gift" | The Greek word δῶρον means both "gift" and "offering" — the ESV picks one sense. The word carries the nuance of something given freely, an offering presented with intention. | "Gift" in modern English can mean something passive or incidental; "offering" carries ritual weight — something deliberately presented at an altar. |
| 3 | "You blind men!" | The Greek τυφλοι is a vocative plural adjective used substantively — "you blind [ones]!" — which is more abstract than "blind men." The blindness is both literal and moral. | The blindness Matthew condemns is not just physical incapacity but willful refusal to see — a moral blindness dressed as innocence. |

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The Altar as Active Agent of Holiness**: The participle "sanctifying" reveals the altar not as a passive object but as an active participant in making offerings holy. The altar doesn't just hold gifts — it confers holiness upon them. This frames the altar as a living center of divine authority, not merely furniture.

2. **The Rhetorical Question as Moral Mirror**: By asking which is greater — the gift or the altar — Matthew forces the listener to confront the relational nature of holiness. A gift without the altar's sanctification is empty; an altar without gifts is purposeless. The question undermines any attempt to separate the offering from its sacred context.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Vocative plural adjective (Adj-VMP)**: τυφloi is an adjective functioning as a vocative noun phrase — "you blind [ones]!" — a common Greek idiom where adjectives stand alone as vocatives.
- **Present active participle (V-PPA-NNS)**: ἁgiazon "sanctifying" is a present participle modifying θúsiaσterion, indicating ongoing action — the altar is in the process of sanctifying, not having completed it.
- **Noun case shift**: δῶron appears first in nominative (the gift as subject) and then in accusative (the gift as object of sanctifying), marking the relational dynamic — the gift is both the thing offered and the thing sanctified.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- This verse belongs to a sequence (23:13-36) of seven "Woes" directed at scribes and Pharisees, each condemning a form of hypocrisy. Verse 19 is part of a sub-sequence (19-22) about oaths, altars, temples, and heaven.
- The altar as a sanctifying agent connects to Exodus 29:37 and 30:29, where the altar's sanctification is treated as a formal ritual act, not a metaphor.
- The ESV's "makes the gift sacred" loses the participle's ongoing sense — sanctification is a living process, not a completed state.

**Cross-References**:

- Exodus 30:29 — "And you shall sanctify them, that they may be most holy..."
- Matthew 5:23-24 — The altar as the locus of offering and sanctification
