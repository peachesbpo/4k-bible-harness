## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Thus also you outwardly indeed (the Greek μὲv is a correlative conjunction meaning "indeed" — it concedes the surface appearance as real before introducing the contrast) appear righteous to men (the Greek ἀvθrωpois literally says "to men" — direct and confrontational, not the softer "to others"), but within, however (the δὲ completes the correlative μὲv...δₑ structure: "indeed...however" — a stronger contrast than the ESV's simple "but"), you are full of hypocrisy (the Greek ὑpoκrisewσ is a privative — literally "answering from below," the deception of responding from a hidden, subordinate position) and of lawlessness (the Greek ἀvomias is a privative — ἀ-vomić = "absence of law/norm" — more specific than KJV's broader "iniquity," which could mean any wrongdoing).

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **From Metaphor to Application**: The word oὖtws ("thus") is a deductive bridge. Verse 23:27 used the tomb metaphor; 23:28 says "Thus also you" — applying the metaphor directly to the Pharisees. You ARE the tombs: outwardly beautiful, within full of hypocrisy and lawlessness. The grammar transforms a comparison into an identity.

2. **Righteousness as Appearance, Not Reality**: The verb φaivésθe (appear) is the same verb used in 23:27 (φaiñoτai) — the Pharisees "appear" righteous as tombs "appear" beautiful. The verb φaivw in Greek carries the sense of seeming rather than being. Michael is making a precise grammatical distinction between appearing righteous and being righteous — the entire hypocrisy charge is encoded in this verb choice.

3. **Hypocrisy and Lawlessness as Paired Privatives**: ὑpoκrisewσ (hypocrisy, Strong's 5272) and ἀvomias (lawlessness, Strong's 458) form a paired charge. Hypocrisy is the deception of answering from a hidden position; lawlessness is the absence of any norm to answer from. Together they describe someone who both deceives about their position AND has no legitimate position to begin with — a double corruption.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Correlative μₑv...δₑ**: Same structure as 23:27 — "outwardly indeed appear righteous, within however are full." The ESV drops the "indeed" entirely, losing the concessive force. The structure is key to the rhetorical impact: it acknowledges the surface appearance before demolishing it.
- **Middle/passive φaivésθe**: The verb φaivw in middle/passive means "to appear/seem" — the Pharisees don't "look righteous" (active) but "appear righteous" (middle/passive). The grammar itself encodes the gap between seeming and being.
- **oὖtws as deductive link**: The adverb oὖtws ("thus") explicitly connects 23:28 to 23:27, transforming the tomb metaphor into a direct accusation. The ESV's "So" is etymologically correct but rhetorically weaker.
