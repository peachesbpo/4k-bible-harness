## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

and you say (the Greek λeγete is "you say" — second person plural, directly addressing the Pharisees; the ESV's "saying" loses the direct address), 'If we had lived in the days of our fathers (the Greek paτepov means "fathers" but carries the sense of "ancestors/those who came before" — predecessors in the faith tradition), we would not have been partakers (the Greek koi vovou means "fellow-partakers/sharers" — those who share in something together; it implies complicity and shared participation, not just "taking part") with them in the blood (the Greek simply says "in the blood of the prophets" — there is no verb for "shedding"; the "shedding" is an interpretive addition in the ESV and NASB; the Greek is more abstract, speaking of sharing in the blood itself, not in the act of shedding it) of the prophets.'

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The Pharisee's Self-Exculpation**: This verse is the Pharisee's spoken defense — they claim that if they had lived in their fathers' days, they wouldn't have joined in "shedding the blood of the prophets." The Greek word koi vovou ("partakers/fellow-sharers") makes this a claim about complicity: the Pharisees are saying they would NOT have been complicit in the sacrifice/suffering of the prophets. The irony is that this very claim — distancing themselves from the prophets' blood — IS the hypocrisy: they build tombs for prophets (23:29) while claiming they wouldn't have shared in the prophets' sacrifice.

2. **Blood as Life and Sacrifice**: The Greek aἵma (blood, Strong's 129, 98 occurrences) is used metaphorically throughout Matthew — "the blood of the covenant" (26:28), "righteous blood shed" (23:35). Here, "the blood of the prophets" means both the prophets' life-blood (their essence, their sanctity) and the shedding of that blood (the sacrifice of the prophets for the sake of the people). The Pharisees claim they wouldn't have shared in this — but building tombs for prophets IS a form of participating in their death, which is the opposite of sharing in their life-blood.

3. **The Counterfactual as Shield**: The verb ἤmeθa (we had lived/we would have been) is in the imperfect indicative — a counterfactual construction. "If we HAD lived...we would NOT have been..." The Pharisees use the counterfactual to distance themselves from the prophets' sacrifice: since they didn't live in their fathers' days, they can't be blamed for not sharing in the prophets' blood. But this is precisely the hypocrisy: they invoke the past as a shield while refusing to honor the prophets' sacrifice in the present.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Counterfactual construction**: The sentence uses a classical Greek counterfactual: Eἰ + imperfect (protasis) ... ouk + imperfect (apodosis). "If we had lived...we would not have been." The imperfect tense in both clauses marks this as a contrary-to-fact conditional — they didn't live in those days, and the claim about what they would have done is unfalsifiable.
- **Dative of participation**: The phrase ἐv τῷ aἵmati ("in the blood") uses the dative case with ἐv to express participation or involvement — literally "in the blood." The Pharisees wouldn't have been "in the blood" of the prophets — wouldn't have been part of the prophets' life-blood/sacrifice.
- **Compound kai + λeγete**: The verse opens with καὶ λeγete ("and you say") — directly continuing from 23:29's comma. The καὶ connects the Pharisees' building (23:29) with their speech (23:30): they build tombs AND they say [this]. The grammar makes building and speaking one continuous act.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- The "blood of the prophets" is a theological category in Matthew's rhetoric — Matthew 23:35 continues this theme ("all the righteous blood shed upon the earth, from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zacharias")
- Matthew 26:28 uses "the blood of the covenant" (aἵma τῆs σvevavsiμ) — "blood" as the life-force of commitment and sacrifice
- The counterfactual construction is the Pharisee's defense: they invoke the past as a shield, claiming they would have acted differently in their fathers' days — but this is precisely the hypocrisy, as they build tombs for prophets in the present while refusing to share in the prophets' sacrifice
