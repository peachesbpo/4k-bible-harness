## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

| # | Standard (ESV) | What's Lost | Why It Matters |
|---|----------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | "even while for a pretence" | The Greek construction here is more nearly "and [even/while] for a pretence" — the ESV's "even while" may over-translate the connective, inserting a concessive/temporal nuance ("even while") that the Greek simple conjunction (kaὶ g2532 "and") does not carry. The KJV's "and" is closer to the Greek. | The ESV implies a simultaneous action with a concessive tone ("even *while*"), but the Greek simply coordinates the two actions: devouring widows' houses AND making long prayers for a pretence. The ESV subtly shifts from coordination to subordination. |
| 2 | "prayes" | This appears to be a textual error — the Greek is singular (proseuxrōmevoi g4336). The ESV's "prayes" is either a typo for "prayers" or an archaic plural, but the Greek singular suggests Jesus means the *practice* of prayer, not individual prayers. | If Jesus means the *making of prayer* (singular abstract) rather than individual prayers (plural), the condemnation is of the entire hypocritical performance, not specific instances. *(Speculative: the singular may reflect Greek idiom rather than a deliberate semantic distinction.)* |

**Note**: The ESV's "condemnation" for Greek κrima (g2917) is reasonable. The KJV's "damnation" is archaic but the Greek root supports "condemn/damn" adequately. No significant loss there.

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **kaτεσθίeτε (g2719)**: "Devour" — a vivid, violent verb. Jesus does not say the scribes "take" or "seize" widows' houses; they *devour* them, like hungry animals consuming prey. The image is deliberately grotesque. The Strong's number g2719 connects this to a root meaning consumption/eating, not merely acquiring.
- **oiκias (g3614)**: "Houses" — from the root oikos (house). These are the homes of the most vulnerable, not just any dwellings. The Strong's number g3614 is the standard word for dwelling/house.
- **χrῶv (g5503)**: "Of widows" — the genitive linking the houses to widows, those who have lost husbands. Jesus specifically targets the most vulnerable in society, making the accusation sharper: scribes don't just take from the rich, they strip the destitute.
- **proφasεi (g4392)**: "For a pretence" — the hypocrites make prayers as a *pretence*, a false show. This word is central to Jesus's rhetorical strategy: he names the sin of pretence explicitly.
- **maκrὰ (g3117)**: "Long" — Jesus specifies that the prayers are *long*, meaning the pretence is elaborate and sustained, not a quick gesture. The hypocrisy is invested in.
- **proseuxrōmevoi (g4336)**: "Make prayers" — the verb for making/producing prayers. Jesus uses the language of *manufacturing* prayer, not *offering* it — as if the prayers are products, not supplications.
- **κrima (g2917)**: "Condemnation/damnation" — the ESV's rendering is good. The Greek carries the sense of being judged/condemned by God, not merely criticized. Jesus is saying these sinners will receive the *verdict* of condemnation from the divine judge.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

This is the **second woe** — Jesus escalates from barring entry (23:13) to active destruction: *devouring* the homes of the most vulnerable. The Greek verb kaτεσθίeτε (g2719, "devour") is deliberately bestial, suggesting hunger and consumption rather than mere theft.

The verse establishes a key theological principle: those who sin under the *pretence* of virtue (making prayers) are worse than ordinary sinners. The "greater condemnation" is not just more blame — it is God's judgment that hypocrisy in the guise of piety deserves harsher verdict than open sin. Jesus is setting up a theological hierarchy of guilt.

The ESV's "even while" (vs. Greek "and" g2532) subtly changes the rhetorical structure from two coordinated sins to one sin qualified by a concessive circumstance — this matters because Jesus is listing two *equal* transgressions, not one with a subordinate clause.

The escalation from 23:13 to 23:14 is architecturally significant: 23:13 says "you shut the door" (passive exclusion), 23:14 says "you devour the houses" (active destruction). The Strong's numbers reveal this: g2808 (shut) → g2719 (devour) — from closing to consuming, from barring to destroying.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- The comparative construction (peisσovτrov g4053 + κrima g2917) creates a "greater...more" structure — Jesus is establishing a graduated scale of condemnation
- The aorist subjunctive kaτεσθíeτε (g2719) places the devouring in the same hypothetical/judgmental register as 23:13's shutting
- The connective kaὶ (g2532, "and") coordinates the two transgressions equally — devouring and making pretence-prayers are paired sins, not one subordinate to the other
- The same structural opener as 23:13: Οὐαὶ (g3759) + ὑμῖv (g5213) + graμμateῖs (g1122) + kaὶ (g2532) + Φarisaῖoi (g5330) + �upokritaί (g5273) — Jesus is using the identical anaphoric structure for each woe

**Cross-References**:
- Verse 14 continues the pattern from 23:13 but escalates from passive exclusion (shutting the kingdom, g2808) to active destruction (devouring widows' houses, g2719)
- The "greater condemnation" (κrima g2917) foreshadows the escalating severity of the subsequent woes
- The "pretence" concept (proφasεi g4392) connects to Jesus's broader theology of hypocrisy — the Pharisee claims virtue while practicing vice
