## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye compass sea and land to make one proselyte; and when he is become so, ye make him twofold more a son of hell than yourselves.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Woe unto you, scribes (literally "letter-writers" — those who traffic in writing) and Pharisees (literally "separatists" — those who hold themselves exclusively apart, religious in name only), hypocrites (literally "stage-players" — actors under an assumed character, dissemblers wearing a mask)! For you lead round and carry about (not merely "compass" — the Greek means to *take someone around as a companion*, to lead them on a circuit) the sea and the arid land (not merely "land" — the Greek word means *dry, withered, earth as opposed to water*, suggesting barrenness and desolation), to make one proselyte (literally "one who has arrived from a foreign region" — an *arriver from outside*, not merely a convert); and when he has become such (the Greek verb means *to cause to be, to come into being* — with "great latitude," suggesting the full generative force of creation, not a passive "is become so"), you make him twofold (literally "two-many" — from *two* + *more/many*) more a son (in the sense of kinship and lineage — one who is *begotten of*, not merely "of") of hell (literally "the valley of Hinnom" — a *place name* in Jerusalem, a geographic metaphor for the place of everlasting punishment) than yourselves.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The theatrical metaphor — hypocrites as stage-players**: The Greek ὑpoκριtaί (g5273) does not merely mean "hypocrite" in the moral sense. It means "an actor under an assumed character" — a *stage-player*, a performer in a costume. The word comes from ὑpokrinomai (to answer from beneath, to reply under-study). Jesus is painting the scribes and Pharisees not merely as liars but as *performers* — actors playing a role they do not inhabit, wearing a costume of piety while being something else entirely. The theatrical image transforms the accusation: these are not just sinners but *imposters in character*, playing a part. The ESV's "hypocrites" names the sin; the Greek paints the sinner.

2. **The separatist sin — Pharisees as those who hold apart**: The Greek Φaρισaῖoi (g5330) literally means "a separatist, i.e. exclusively religious." The word encodes the very sin Jesus is condemning: these are people who *separate themselves* from others, who hold themselves *exclusively* apart. The Pharisee is a separatist by definition — and Jesus's entire pericope is about people who exclude others (23:13: "ye shut the kingdom... against men") while holding themselves in isolated privilege. The word itself is the diagnosis.

3. **The geographic metaphor — hell as a valley in Jerusalem**: The Greek γέénnis (g1067) — geéenna — is literally "the valley of Hinnom," a place name in Jerusalem. It is a *geographic metaphor* for the place (or state) of everlasting punishment. Jesus is not saying the proselyte is spiritually "of hell" in an abstract sense; he is saying the proselyte is *from the Valley* — a specific, topographic location of damnation. The image is spatial: the proselyte has been *placed* in a valley, a depression, the lowest geographic point. And as a "son" (υἱὸv, g5207) of that valley, the proselyte is its *kin* — begotten of it, an heir of the place of punishment. The ESV's "son of hell" flattens this into a common metaphor, losing the topographic specificity and the sense of *belonging to a place*.

4. **The generative force — "become" as creation, not change**: The Greek γένetai (g1096) — gínomai — means "to cause to be, to generate, to come into being" with "great latitude." Jesus is not saying the proselyte has merely *changed* or *been converted*; he is saying the proselyte has been *generated* — brought into being as a new creature. The word carries the sense of creation ex nihilo: the proselyte was made, *generated*, produced. This generative force matters because the accusation is not "you changed someone" but "you *created* a new being — and then made that being twofold worse." The Pharisees are not corrupting existing sinners; they are *manufacturing* new ones from raw material.

5. **The barren land — ξηpὰv as "arid, withered"**: The Greek word for "land" in this verse is not the generic word for earth or ground but ξηpὰv (g3584) — xērós — meaning "arid; by implication, shrunken, withered." LSJ explicitly cross-references this verse: "η ξηρά (sc. γῆ), dry land, opp. ὑγρα, cf. Ev.Matt.23.15." The opposition is between ὑγρα (water, the living element) and ξηρά (dry land, the withered element). Jesus is saying the Pharisees search both the flowing sea AND the barren, desolate earth — the entire reach of the world, both its living and dead regions. The barrenness of the land mirrors the futility of the search: they scour even the withered places for a single proselyte.

6. **The mathematical precision of "twofold"**: The Greek διplôteρov (g1362) — diploûs — is etymologically δίς (two) + πλείov (more/many). Jesus is not saying "much more" or "far more" but *exactly double*. The proselyte is precisely twofold more a son of hell than his converter. This mathematical precision elevates the accusation from rhetoric to geometric moral logic: hypocrisy is not just self-corruption but a *doubling* sin — each convert becomes exactly twice the son of hell that his converter is. The compounding is precise, not vague.

**Grammatical Observations**:

- **The �ti...�tan structure (causal→temporal shift)**: The verse opens with �ti (g3754, "because/for") introducing the causal clause — woe unto you BECAUSE you compass sea and land. Then �tan (g3752, "when/whenever") shifts to a temporal clause — AND WHEN he has become such. This two-stage structure is rhetorically powerful: first the effort (the exhaustive search), then the consequence (the doubling). The ESV preserves this with "for... when" but the shift from causal to temporal registers deserves attention — Jesus is not just listing sins but constructing a temporal sequence: first you search, then you convert, then you corrupt.

- **The aorist subjunctive poiεῖte (g4160)**: The second person plural aorist subjunctive — "ye make" — is the same prohibitive/precative mood seen in v8 (klēthēte). But here it's not a prohibition directed at Jesus's disciples; it's a declaration *about* the Pharisees' actions. The subjunctive mood marks the action as hypothetical/judgmental — Jesus is not describing what the Pharisees did but what they *would do*, what their nature *leads them to do*. The mood places the accusation in the register of judgment, not mere description.

- **perisγete (g4013) as "lead around, take about"**: The verb is not merely "travel" or "traverse" but *to lead someone around as a companion, to carry about*. The Pharisees are not just searching; they are *drawing others along* on a circuit. The accusative objects (the sea and the arid land) are what they lead around — they conduct their search across the entirety of the earth, both its living waters and barren grounds.

- **The contrast θaλassav vs. ξηpὰv (sea vs. arid land)**: LSJ explicitly cross-references this verse for the opposition �upρa (water) vs. ξηpὰv (dry land). The contrast is not between two neutral geography terms but between the *living, flowing element* (the sea) and the *withered, barren element* (the arid land). Jesus is saying the Pharisees search both the fertile and the desolate reaches of the earth — the entire world, in both its living and dead forms.

- **The compound διplôteρov (g1362) — "two-fold"**: The etymology δίς (two) + πλείov (more/many) shows Jesus constructing the doubling *mathematically*. This is not a vague intensifier ("much more") but a precise calculation: *two* + *more* = exactly double. The proselyte is precisely twofold more a son of hell than his converter. The mathematical precision elevates the accusation from rhetoric to geometric moral logic.

- **The reflexive aὐtὸv (g846) — "him(self)"**: The reflexive pronoun marks the proselyte as the object of the Pharisees' making. They make *him* — the one who has been converted — into something worse. The reflexive force suggests the proselyte is made *in their image* but distorted, amplified, doubled.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:

- This is the **third woe** in the series (23:13, 23:14, 23:15). The first woe (23:13) condemns shutting the kingdom — passive exclusion. The second woe (23:14) condemns devouring widows' houses — active destruction of the vulnerable. The third woe (23:15) escalates to the *manufacture of new sinners* — the Pharisees don't just sin themselves; they *create* converts and make those converts *twofold worse*. The escalation is from passive exclusion → active destruction → multiplicative corruption.

- The "valley of Hinnom" (γέénnis, g1067) is a place name of Hebrew origin (H01516, H02011). In Jerusalem, the Valley of Hinnom (Ge-Hinnom) was associated with everlasting punishment — it was the place where the damned were sent. Jesus's use of this place name as a metaphor for "hell" is not merely figurative but *topographic*: the proselyte is placed in a *valley*, a geographic depression, the lowest point. The image is spatial and concrete, not abstract.

- The anaphoric structure (Οὐαὶ �upμivv,γραmmaτεῖs kaὶ Φa risaῖoi, �upokritaί) is identical to 23:13 and 23:14 — Jesus uses the same opening for each woe, creating a rhetorical series. But each woe adds a new accusation: 23:13 = shutting, 23:14 = devouring, 23:15 = manufacturing and doubling. The series builds in severity.

- The "twofold" (διplôteρov) multiplication foreshadows Jesus's broader theological point: hypocrisy is a *compounding* sin. Each convert becomes a new vector of the original sin, and at twice the intensity. The mathematical precision suggests Jesus sees hypocrisy not as a fixed sin but as an *exponential* one — each generation of converts doubles the corruption.
