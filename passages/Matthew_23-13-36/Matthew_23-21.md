## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

And the one having sworn by the temple swears by it and by the one dwelling in it. (The Greek uses καtoikoῦnτi, a present participle "dwelling" — not "who dwells" as a relative clause. The participle form carries the sense of ongoing, continuous inhabitation: the dweller is not merely present but actively living within the temple. The ESV's "him who dwells" is accurate but loses the participial emphasis on ongoing inhabitation as a defining characteristic of the temple's sacredness. The ESV also capitalizes "Him" implying God, but the Greek has no capitalization — it is "the one dwelling," which could be God or any authorized dweller.)

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The Temple as Sanctified by Dwelling**: The key shift from 23:20 (altar) to 23:21 (temple) is that the altar binds through objects upon it, while the temple binds through the person dwelling within it. Sanctification by the altar is about what rests on it; sanctification by the temple is about who lives in it. The temple's holiness comes from inhabitation, not from objects.

2. **The Escalation from Object to Person**: The sequence altar → temple → heaven escalates from inanimate sanctification (altar/gifts) to personal sanctification (temple/dweller) to divine sanctification (heaven/throne). Each step raises the stakes of the oath — swearing by an altar binds you to objects; swearing by a temple binds you to a person; swearing by heaven binds you to God Himself.

3. **Dwelling as Active Inhabitation**: The participle katoikoῦnτi (Strong's 2730, 45 occurrences) is consistently used across Matthew for "living in" or "settling in" — not merely being present but making a home. The temple's sanctifier is someone who has settled there, who lives there as their dwelling. This implies permanence and commitment, not casual presence.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Parallel structure with 23:20**: The verse mirrors 23:20 exactly in grammatical structure (ὁ ὀmόσas ἐn τῷ [object] ὀmnύei ἐn aὐtῷ kaὶ ἐn [what it binds]) — only the object changes from altar to temple, and the binding target from "all things upon it" to "the one dwelling in it."
- **Participle of inhabitation (V-PPA-DMS)**: kaτoiκoῦnτi is a present active participle in the dative/genitive case, modifying τῷ (the one). It describes not just residence but active, ongoing living — the dweller has settled and continues to dwell.
- **Variant texts**: The Byzantine Majority Text (2005) and Greek Orthodox Church editions use kaτoikηsaνti instead of kaτoikoῦnτi — a different stem (κατoikeσaν instead of kaτoiκoῦnτ), but the same meaning ("dwelling/living"). This suggests a textual variant in the root, not a semantic difference.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- The cross-references for this verse (1 Kings 8:13, 2 Chronicles 6:2, Psalm 26:8, Psalm 132:13, Ephesians 2:22, Colossians 2:9) all relate to "habitation" — dwelling in a place as a form of divine presence. The concept of temple-as-dwelling-place is central to the NT's theology of God's habitation.
- The temple (naῷ) in NT theology is distinct from the altar (θúsiaσterion): the altar sanctifies objects placed upon it, while the temple sanctifies through the one who dwells within it. The escalation from altar to temple marks a shift from object-sanctification to person-sanctification.
- Colossians 2:9 ("in him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily") connects the temple-dweller to the indwelling of God's fullness — the dweller embodies God's presence.
