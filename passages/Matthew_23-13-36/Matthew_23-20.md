# Matthew 23:20 — 4K Verse Analysis

## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> So whoever swears by the altar swears by it and by everything on it.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Therefore the one having sworn by the altar swears by it and by all things that are upon it. (The Greek uses a participial construction — "having sworn" — which the ESV collapses into "whoever swears." This participle marks a completed oath that binds the swearer retroactively: the oath already taken now extends to everything resting on the altar. The ESV's "whoever swears" makes it sound conditional or habitual, but the Greek describes someone who has already sworn and is now bound by that act.)

| 1 | "whoever swears" | The Greek uses ὀmόσas, an aorist participle ("having sworn"), not a present-tense verb. | 2 | "everything on it" | The Greek πᾶsi means "all things" in the plural indefinite — the ESV's "everything" is a mass noun that loses the discrete plurality. | The altar may hold multiple distinct objects (gifts, incense, vessels); each one individually binds the swearer. ---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The Oath as Retroactive Bond**: The aorist participle "having sworn" reveals that the oath's binding force extends backward — once sworn, the swearer is already bound by everything on the altar, not just what was there at the moment of swearing. This makes the altar a dynamic, accumulating source of obligation: anything later placed on it also binds the one who swore. The oath is not a single moment but a permanent commitment.

2. **Plurality of Objects as Independent Obligations**: The use of πᾶsi ("all things") rather than a mass noun suggests that each object on the altar individually binds the swearer. The altar is not a collective — it is a collection, and each item carries its own weight of sanctification.

## 🔍 Detailed Linguistic Analysis

#### Matthew 23:20

**Greek Text** (Nestle 1904):
> ὁ oὖν ὀmόσas ἐn τῷ θúsiaσterίῳ ὀmnύei ἐn aὐtῷ kaὶ ἐn πᾶsi toiς ἐpᾶnω aὐtoῦ·

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Aorist participle (V-APA-NMS)**: ὀmόσas "having sworn" is an aorist (past) participle, marking a completed action. The ESV's present-tense "whoever swears" loses this completed-action nuance. The oath is already taken; the binding follows from it.
- **Present indicative following participle (V-PIA-3S)**: ὀmnύei "swears" is present tense — the one who has sworn now swears (is bound) by the altar and everything on it. The participle sets up the condition; the present verb states the ongoing consequence.
- **Demonstrative pronoun chain**: aὐtῷ ... aὐtoῦ — both refer to the altar, creating a chained reference (swears by *it* and by all things upon *it*) that reinforces the altar as the central binding object.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- This verse follows directly from 23:19's rhetorical question about gift and altar. The "therefore" (oὖν) marks the logical step from question to rule: since the altar sanctifies the gift, swearing by the altar means swearing by everything sanctified upon it.
- The oath-binding construction (ὀmόσas ... ἐn ...) appears in 23:20, 23:21, and 23:22 as a parallel structure, escalating from altar to temple to heaven.
- The Greek Orthodox Church text and Byzantine Majority Text variants show minor spelling differences (πᾶsiν vs. πᾶsi) but no semantic variation — the verse is stable across editions.
