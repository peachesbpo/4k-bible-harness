### Matthew 23:13

**Greek Text**: ❌ Issues found

The file's Greek text has **10 character differences** vs verified source. Most are Greek-vs-Latin encoding inconsistencies (same letter, different Unicode codepoint), but two are **substantive text differences**:

| Position | Verified | File | Type |
|----------|----------|------|------|
| 14–19 | `graμμateῖs` (Greek μμ, Greek α) | `grammaτεῖs` (Latin mm, Latin a) | Encoding inconsistency |
| 23 | `ς` (Greek final sigma) | `s` (Latin s) | Encoding inconsistency |
| 58 | `e` (Latin e) | `ε` (Greek epsilon) | Encoding inconsistency |
| **67** | **`ὰ` (U+1F70)** | **`ὴ` (U+1F74)** | **Substantive — different Greek vowel** |
| **68** | **`ς` (U+03C2, final sigma)** | **`v` (U+0076, Latin v)** | **Substantive — different character entirely** |

The word `τὰς` (verified) appears as `τὴv` in the file. This is the word rendered as "of" in the translation (as in "of the kingdom"). The verified has `τὰς` (tau + o-with-macron-below + final-sigma) while the file has `τὴv` (tau + o-with-tilde-below + v). These are genuinely different characters, not just encoding variants.

**Diffs**:
- Diff #1: ❌ **Overstated / etymological commentary**. The diff claims ᅦti carries a causal-temporal nexus and that the ESV's "because" captures it well — but then the "Why It Matters" column explains that the ESV is actually *better* than the KJV. If nothing is lost in the ESV, this doesn't belong in a "What Got Lost?" table. It's etymological commentary about ᅦti dressed up as a diff. The file even says "No significant translation loss detected beyond the nuance noted above." This should be moved to Etymological Notes.

**Strong's**: ✅ All entries marked "(unverified)" — honest. Plausible transliterations.

**Corrections needed**:
1. Fix Greek text: `τὴv` → `τὰς` (positions 67–68) to match verified source
2. Fix encoding inconsistencies: `grammaτεῖs` → `graμμateῖs` (or standardize — see note below)
3. Move Diff #1 to Etymological Notes — it's commentary, not a translation gap

## Cross-Cutting Observations

### Encoding Inconsistency
The Greek texts use a mixed Greek/Latin transliteration system where the same letter is sometimes represented by its Greek Unicode codepoint and sometimes by its Latin equivalent. This affects 23:13 (most severely), 23:14, 23:15, and 23:16. 23:17 and 23:18 are clean. The files should standardize on one encoding approach. The verified source text from bible-api should be treated as canonical.

### Strong's Numbers
Every Strong's entry across all 6 files is marked "(unverified)." This is honest and appropriate — no Strong's numbers are being fabricated or claimed without verification. No action needed beyond noting that verification would strengthen the analyses.

### Pattern of Honest Self-Assessment
A strength of these files is that several of them explicitly acknowledge when the ESV is accurate:
- 23:13: "The ESV is substantially accurate for this verse"
- 23:15: "The ESV is substantially accurate for this verse"
- 23:16: "The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse"
- 23:18: "The ESV accurately captures this verse"

This is good analytical practice. The main issue is that some "diffs" are commentary rather than translation gaps, and should be reclassified.
