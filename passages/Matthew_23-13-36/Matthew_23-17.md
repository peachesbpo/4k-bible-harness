## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Ye fools and blind: for which is greater, the gold, or the temple that hath sanctified the gold?

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

You fools and blind: for which is greater, the gold, or the temple that has sanctified the gold?

---

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

**The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected.**

The ESV's "which" vs. KJV's "whether" for Greek 👣aρ is a stylistic preference rather than a translation gap. "Which is greater" is a perfectly good rhetorical question in English — the rhetorical force comes from context and the question mark, not from the choice of "which" vs. "whether."

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **Mρoi (Mpoi)**: "Fools" — Jesus adds "fools" to "blind," escalating the accusation. The blind guides are not just unable to see — they are *foolish* in their blindness. The combination is devastating: willfully ignorant and proud of it.
- **kaὶ τuλλoi (kai tulloi)**: "And blind" — Jesus keeps "blind" from the previous verse, maintaining continuity while adding "fools" as an escalation.
- **γaρ (gap)**: "Which/whether" — the interrogative particle. The Greek carries a rhetorical force that "whether" in English captures better than "which" — it implies "let us weigh and see."
- **meizov (meizon)**: "Greater" — the comparative. Jesus is asking the reader to *weigh* gold against temple, and the answer is self-evident: the temple is greater because it sanctifies the gold. The gold cannot sanctify itself.
- **χrustos (chrustos)**: "Gold" — continuing the metaphor from 23:16. The gold is the material content; without the temple it has no sanctification.
- **vaosi (vaosi)**: "Temple" — the sacred structure that *sanctifies* the gold. Jesus is making a profound theological point: value flows from the sacred structure to the material content, not the reverse.
- **tov χrustov (tov chrustov)**: "That hath sanctified the gold" — the verb "sanctify" is key. Jesus is saying the temple *conferred sanctity* upon the gold. Without the temple, the gold is just metal.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

This is the **fifth woe** (though it functions as a rhetorical question following the fourth). Jesus is pressing the temple/gold metaphor to its logical conclusion: if the temple sanctifies the gold, then the temple is *greater* than the gold — and to swear by the lesser (gold) while ignoring the greater (temple) is not just foolish but spiritually incoherent.

The theological insight is about the *direction of sanctification*: sanctity flows from the sacred *form* to the material *content*, never the reverse. The temple makes the gold meaningful; the gold does not make the temple sacred. Jesus is exposing the spiritual error of those who value material wealth over the sacred structures that give wealth its meaning.

The Greek rhetorical question (γaρ meizov) is not a genuine inquiry but a *reductio ad absurdum*: the answer is so obvious that the blind guides' position collapses. The ESV's "which" slightly weakens this rhetorical force compared to the KJV's "whether," but both convey the essential meaning.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- The interrogative γaρ (which/whether) governs the entire clause — it is the rhetorical pivot of the verse
- The nominative case of both χrustos and vaosi places gold and temple in direct grammatical opposition — they are the two candidates being weighed
- The relative clause ᅦo tov χrustov ("that sanctified the gold") is the theological key: it establishes the *causal direction* — temple sanctifies gold, not vice versa
- The verse ends with a question mark in Greek, confirming its interrogative (rhetorical) force

**Cross-References**:
- This verse directly follows 23:16 as a rhetorical challenge to the blind guides' own principle — if they say "swear by the gold," Jesus asks: which is greater, the gold or the temple that made the gold sacred?
- The temple/gold contrast from 23:16 reaches its logical conclusion here
- The "fools and blind" escalation sets up 23:18, where Jesus extends the metaphor to altar and gifts
