## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye tithe mint and anise and cummin, and have left undone the weightier matters of the law, justice, and mercy, and faith: but these ye ought to have done, and not to have left the other undone.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Woe unto you (an exclamation of pain and anger — not just "alas" but the cry of grief itself), scribes (and "scribes" means secretaries, registrars, subordinate officials — a word sometimes used as a term of contempt) and Pharisees (and "Pharisees" means separatists — those who have made themselves exclusively religious, a sect apart), hypocrites (and "hypocrites" literally means stage-players — actors under an assumed character, performing a role rather than living a truth)! For ye tithe (and "tithe" literally means to take a tenth part — a precise mathematical term, not a casual verb) mint and anise and cummin (three specific spices — the trifles you attend to with exacting precision), and have omitted (not merely "left undone" as if by accident, but deliberately passed over) the weightier (and "weightier" is comparative — not just weighty but heavier, heavier than the trifles you strain at) matters of the law (and "the law" here is the same word used for divine ordinance), judgment (and "judgment" means decision, tribunal, JUSTICE — especially, divine law itself), and mercy, and faith: but these ye ought (and "ought" means it is binding, necessary — not a suggestion but a moral obligation) to have done, and not to have left the other undone (the other — meaning the trifles too; you should have done both, but you have done only the small things and left the great things undone).

### Concepts Flattened

- **Contemptuous force of "scribes"**: Flattened from a term of contempt (petty official, clerk) to a neutral archaic word.
- **Separatism in "Pharisees"**: Flattened from a word that encodes separatism (exclusively religious, a sect apart) to a proper name.
- **Theatrical metaphor in "hypocrites"**: Flattened from "stage-player, actor under an assumed character" to a moral term. The theatrical metaphor — performing rather than living — is lost.
- **Mathematical precision of "tithe"**: Flattened from a literal mathematical operation (take a tenth) to a general verb about paying.
- **Comparative force of "weightier"**: Flattened from comparative (heavier than) to positive (weighty). The relative weight — heavier than the trifles — is the whole point.
- **Divine law in "judgment"**: Flattened from JUSTICE and DIVINE LAW to "judgment" (opinion). The weight of what they neglected is lost.
- **Deontic force of "ought"**: Flattened from "binding/necessary" to a softer "should." The moral obligation loses its force.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The three labels form a descending hierarchy of contempt**: Jesus names them as scribes (petty officials, clerks — a term of contempt), then Pharisees (separatists — those who made themselves exclusively religious, a sect apart), then hypocrites (stage-players — actors performing a character). The hierarchy descends from professional insignificance to ideological separatism to outright fraud. The same people are all three: petty clerks who became a sect who became actors playing a part.

2. **"Tithe" as mathematical exactitude**: Jesus doesn't say "you pay" or "you give." He says you TITHE — you take a precise tenth part. The Pharisees are mathematically exact about trifles. They compute their tenth of mint, anise, and cummin with bookkeeping precision. The mathematical term elevates the critique: these people aren't just generous with small things, they are METICULOUS about them — and yet they omit justice, mercy, and faith.

3. **"Judgment" = justice and divine law**: The Greek word κpίsis doesn't mean "judgment" as opinion. It means JUSTICE — and the Strong's definition explicitly says "especially, divine law." When Jesus says they've left undone "judgment," he means they've left undone JUSTICE ITSELF — the divine law that should govern all their conduct. The ESV's "judgment" sounds like they neglected to form opinions. The Greek says they neglected JUSTICE.

4. **The comparative "weightier" is the whole critique**: The Greek is comparative — HEAVIER, not just heavy. Jesus isn't saying the matters of the law are weighty in themselves. He's saying they're heavier THAN the trifles the Pharisees attend to. The relative weight IS the critique: you tend to the light things and omit the heavy things. The comparative makes the inversion of priorities explicit.

5. **"Ought" as binding necessity**: The Greek ἔδeι isn't advice. It's a deontic verb — it binds. Jesus declares a moral obligation: you OUGHT to have done these things. The force is not "it would be advisable" but "it is necessary, it is binding." Jesus speaks as one declaring what MUST be — not suggesting but commanding.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Vocative chain (scribes, Pharisees, hypocrites)**: All three are in the vocative case — Jesus is addressing them directly, naming them to their faces. The chain descends from professional insignificance (scribe/clerk) to ideological separatism (Pharisee/separatist) to outright fraud (hypocrite/stage-player). The same people are all three.
- **Comparative adjective (βapύτepa, g926)**: The comparative — "heavier" — is essential. Jesus isn't saying the matters are weighty in themselves. He's saying they're heavier THAN the trifles. The comparative makes the priority inversion explicit: these weigh more than those.
- **Deontic verb (ἔδeι, g1163)**: The "ought" is deontic — it binds. Not "it would be good" but "it is necessary." Jesus speaks as one declaring a moral law, not offering advice.
- **Causal conjunction (�oti, g3754)**: The �oti that opens the explanation means "because" — Jesus is giving the REASON for the woe. Woe unto you BECAUSE you tithe trifles and omit the weightier matters. The causal force connects the grief to the specific sin.
- **Three spices as a list**: The three spices (mint, anise, cummin) are listed with caὶ (and) between each — a polysyndetic construction that emphasizes each item individually. Jesus names each trifle separately to emphasize how many small things they attend to while omitting the great things.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- This verse returns to the opening of the Seven Woes pericope. Verse 1 opened with "Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!" — and v23 repeats the exact same opening. But v1 was a general indictment; v23 specifies the sin: you tithe trifles and omit the weightier matters of the law.
- The three weightier matters — judgment, mercy, faith — form the same triad that Micah 6:8 echoes: "do justly, and love mercy, and walk humbly with thy God." The cross-reference (23 votes, the strongest match) confirms this is a deliberate echo of the same divine law.
- The LSJ classical lexicon cites this passage for several words: ἄveθov (anise) at "Ev.Matt.23.23" and ὑpokpίτas (hypocrite) at "Ev.Matt.23.13" (the verse numbering discrepancy in the source data — v13 vs v23 — is a known issue with the bible-api Greek source). These are canonical example passages for these words.

**Cross-References**:
- **Micah 6:8** (23 votes) — "He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the Lord require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?" — The OT parallel: judgment = do justly, mercy = love mercy, faith = walk humbly with thy God. The same triad, the same divine law.
