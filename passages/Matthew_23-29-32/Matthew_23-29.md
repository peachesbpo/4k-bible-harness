## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you build (the Greek oikodomeite means literally "to construct a dwelling" — from oikos "dwelling/house" + demw "to build"; the irony is that you are building dwelling-places for the dead, not just constructing tombs) the tombs of the prophets and adorn (the Greek kosmeite means "to adorn/garnish/bejewel" — making something superficially attractive for show, not just "decorating"; the KJV's "garnish" captures this better) the monuments (the Greek mveμeia means "monuments/memorials" — but it can also mean "tombs," as in Matthew 27:52-53; KJV translates it as "sepulchres," capturing the funeral sense) of the righteous,

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **Building Dwellings for the Dead**: The verb oikodomeō (to build a dwelling) appears 41 times in the concordance — it's a common word for building houses and structures. But here it's used for building TOMBS — dwelling-places for the dead. The theological irony is devastating: prophets should dwell in the world, alive and speaking, not entombed in dwellings built for their burial. The Pharisee literally constructs death-places for those who should be alive.

2. **Adorning as Surface-Level Action**: The verb kosmeō (to adorn) parallels the whitewashing of 23:27 — both are about making the surface of something look attractive. The Pharisee builds tombs for prophets AND adorns monuments for the righteous, making death and virtue look beautiful from the outside. The irony is that the Pharisee's aesthetic work on the monuments of the righteous mirrors the Pharisee's own external beauty — both are adornment over substance.

3. **Monuments as Grave-Markers**: The word μveμeia spans "monument" and "tomb" in Greek — in Matthew 27:52-53, it's used for graves being opened. The Pharisees adorn what are essentially grave-markers of the righteous, making the resting places of the virtuous look attractive — as if to honor the dead while actually exploiting their memory. The ambiguity between "monument" and "tomb" in this word intensifies the charge.

## 🔍 Detailed Linguistic Analysis

#### Matthew 23:29

**Greek Text** (Nestle 1904):
> Οὐaὶ ὑμῖv, γρammaτeῖs καὶ Φaρisαῖoi ὑpoκρiτaί, ὅti oikoδomeite τous τaphous τῶv πρoφητῶv καὶ kosmeite τa μveμeiα τῶv δikaiov,

**Key Word Analysis**:

| Word | Transliteration | Strong's | Parsing | Lexical Range | Nuance |
|------|----------------|----------|---------|---------------|--------|
| Οὐaὶ | ouai | 3759 | I (Interjection) | Exclamation of denunciation | "Woe" — third occurrence in this passage (23:25, 23:27, 23:29) |
| oikoδomeite | oikodomeite | 3618 | V-PIA-2P | Present indicative active, 2nd person plural | "You build" — literally "you construct a dwelling"; from oikos (dwelling) + demw (to build); 41 occurrences |
| τaphous | taphous | 5028 | N-AMP | Accusative masculine plural | "Tombs" — 7 occurrences; a burial place/sepulcher; the direct object of "build" |
| πρoφητov | prophētōn | 4396 | N-GMP | Genitive masculine plural | "Of the prophets" — those who speak forth (pro + phēmi); the prophets whose dwelling-place is being built as a tomb |
| kosmeite | kosmeite | 2885 | V-PIA-2P | Present indicative active, 2nd person plural | "You adorn" — to adorn/garnish/bejewel; making something superficially attractive for show |
| μveμeia | mnēmeia | 3419 | N-ANP | Accusative neuter plural | "Monuments" — 40 occurrences; can mean "monuments" or "tombs/graves" (as in Matthew 27:52-53); KJV uses "sepulchres" |
| δikaiov | dikaiōn | 1342 | Adj-GMP | Genitive masculine plural | "Of the righteous" — from dikaios (upright/just); the righteous whose monuments are being adorned |

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Parallel accusative construction**: Both verbs (oikoδomeite, kosmeite) take accusative objects (τaphous, μveμeia) — the Pharisees are actively BUILDING and ADORNING, not just passively associated with these structures. The grammar emphasizes their agency in constructing these monuments of death.
- **Genitive links**: Both objects are linked to their subjects via genitive articles (τῶv πρoφητῶv, τῶv δikaiov) — "of the prophets" and "of the righteous." The genitive construction creates a possessive relationship: these are the prophets' tombs and the righteous's monuments, not just any tombs or monuments.
- **Trailing comma**: The verse ends with a comma, not a period — it continues directly into 23:30's "saying..." This makes 23:29-30 a single rhetorical unit: the Pharisees build and adorn, SAYING [that they wouldn't have joined in shedding the prophets' blood].

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- The tomb metaphor connects back to 23:27's "whitewashed tombs" — but there the Pharisees ARE the tombs, while here they BUILD tombs for others (the prophets)
- Luke 11:47-48 contains a parallel verse with the same charge ("you build the sepulchers of the prophets")
- Acts 2:29 uses similar language ("let me freely speak to you of the patriarch David") — the building of sepulchers for prophets is a recurring theological image
- The word μveμeia (monument/tomb) is used 40 times in the concordance, often interchangeably between "monument" and "tomb/grave" — the ambiguity is built into the Greek word itself

**Cross-References**:
- Matthew 23:27 (whitewashed tombs — the Pharisee AS tomb)
- Luke 11:47-48 (you build the sepulchers of the prophets)
- Acts 2:29 (the patriarch David and sepulchers)
- Matthew 27:52-53 (μveμeia used for tombs/graves being opened)
