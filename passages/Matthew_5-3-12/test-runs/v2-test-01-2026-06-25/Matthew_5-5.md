## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed are the meek (Greek πraύς — literally "mild, gentle," but carrying a specific classical valence: the word was used of a horse that had been tamed, of animals that had been brought to gentleness, and of persons who were gentle *especially after having been angry* — the connotation is not weakness but rather a calming of strength, a bringing-into-gentleness, as a bridle tames a horse: the power is still there, but it has been settled, made mild): for they shall inherit (Greek κληpovouησou — "to be an heir to," a legal term of inheritance and heirship, not merely receiving but being the rightful heir, the one to whom something is bequeathed by legacy) the earth (Greek γῆ — "soil, land, the solid part or the whole of the terrene globe, including the occupants in each application" — not just the ground but the world and everyone on it).

## 📝 Etymological Notes

- **πraύς (g4239)**: Strong's derivation: "apparently a primary word." Strong's definition: "mild, i.e. (by implication) humble." KJV gloss: "meek." LSJ classical dictionary shows it used of things (mild, soft, gentle), of persons (mild, gentle, meek, *especially after having been angry*), of horses (gentle, tame), and of other animals (tame). The LSJ also records a causative sense: "making mild, taming" — used of a bridle. The word's semantic category in bible-db is `emotion_mind>attitude_character`. The connotation is not absence of strength but *settled* strength — strength brought to gentleness, as a wild horse is tamed and bridled.

- **κληpovouησou (g2816)**: Strong's derivation: "from κλepovouoς (heir)." Strong's definition: "to be an heir to (literally or figuratively)." KJV gloss: "be heir, (obtain by) inherit(-ance)." LSJ shows it used with both genitive (inheriting from a person) and accusative (obtaining/acquiring something), with the core meaning of legal inheritance and heirship. Categories: `action>give_take` (primary), `society_politics>commerce` (secondary). The word carries formal/legal weight — this is bequeathal, not mere receipt.

- **γῆ (g1093)**: Strong's derivation: "contracted from a primary word." Strong's definition: "soil; by extension a region, or the solid part or the whole of the terrene globe (including the occupants in each application)." KJV gloss: "country, earth(-ly), ground, land, world." Categories: `nature>earth_stone` (primary), `place>landform` (secondary). The Strong's definition's parenthetical — "including the occupants in each application" — expands the meaning beyond mere ground to the world as a living, inhabited place.

- **Μaκariot (g3107)**: The signature Beatitude word. Strong's: "supremely blest; by extension, fortunate, well off." LSJ shows it meaning blessed, happy, prosperous — and notably *distinct from euδaιmov* (freed from suffering). The word carries the sense of supreme blessing, not mere happiness. Categories: `abstract_quality>state_condition` (primary), `emotion_mind>emotion` (secondary).

- **ὅti (g3754)**: Causal conjunction — "because, for, that." Strong's derivation: "neuter of ὅσtiς as conjunction." Category: `function_word>conjunction`. Appears in every Beatitude verse as the structural hinge connecting the blessing to its promise.

- **αὐtoὶ (g846)**: Strong's: "the reflexive pronoun self, used of the third person." But in this context it functions as the personal pronoun "they" — the LSJ confirms this oblique-case usage for the third person. Category: `function_word>pronoun`.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **Gentled strength, not weakness**: The Greek πraύς (g4239) means "mild, gentle" — but the LSJ classical usage reveals it was used of horses that had been tamed, of animals brought to gentleness, and of persons gentle *especially after having been angry*. Jesus is blessing those whose strength has been *settled* — not those who never had strength. The theological claim is that tamed, bridled, gentled power — strength exercised with restraint — is worthy of inheriting the earth. The ESV "meek" risks reading as praising weakness, when the Greek supports praising disciplined strength.

2. **Inheritance as bequeathal, not conquest**: The use of κλepovouησou (g2816) — "to be an heir to" — frames the earth as a legacy, not a prize to be won. The meek do not conquer the earth; they inherit it. The earth is bequeathed to them by those who came before, passed down by right of inheritance. This reframes the meek as heirs — those who receive by legacy, not by force.

3. **The earth as inhabited world, not empty land**: The Strong's definition of γῆ (g1093) includes "the whole of the terrene globe (including the occupants in each application)." Jesus is not just giving the meek land or territory — he is giving them the entire world with everyone on it. The earth as inheritance means stewardship of the whole human enterprise: the meek inherit not just soil but people, the whole living world.

4. **Parallel with 5:3**: Verse 5:3 says the poor in spirit possess the kingdom of heaven; 5:5 says the meek (the gentled, the tamed) inherit the earth. Together, they claim that both heavenly and earthly dominion belong to the humble — those who restrain themselves, not those who assert themselves. The kingdom of heaven is *possessed* by the poor in spirit; the earth is *inherited* by the meek. Both are framed as passive receipt by the humble, not active conquest by the strong.

5. **The Beatitude structure — blessedness as supreme state**: The word Μaκariot (g3107) means "supremely blest" — not just happy or content but *supremely* blessed. The LSJ distinguishes it from euδaιmov (freed from suffering) — makarios is a higher, more absolute state. Jesus is not merely wishing the meek well; he is declaring them supremely blessed, in the highest sense the word can carry.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **πraύς (g4239)** is an adjective in the nominative plural, functioning substantivally: "the mild/gentle ones." The Strong's derivation notes it is "apparently a primary word" — not derived from another root. Its semantic category `emotion_mind>attitude_character` places it in the domain of disposition and character, not physical weakness.
- **κlηpovouησouσiv (g2816)** is a future verbal form — "they shall inherit." The future sense conveys a promise of what shall be, not what currently is. Its categories (`action>give_take` + `society_politics>commerce`) reflect the legal/formal weight of inheritance.
- **τὴv γῆv** is in the accusative case — "the earth" as the direct object of "inherit." The primary category `nature>earth_stone` with secondary `place>landform` shows the word spans physical ground and geographic world.
- The verse follows the standard Beatitude structure: [Blessed] + [those with a quality] + [for they shall receive a blessing].

**Cross-References**:
- **Maκariot** (g3107) — the signature Beatitude word, appearing in all 10 verses. LSJ distinguishes it from euδaιmov (freed from suffering) — makarios is supreme blessedness, a higher state.
- **ὅti** (g3754) — the causal conjunction, appearing in every Beatitude. Derives from ὅσtiς as conjunction.
- **αὐtoὶ** (g846) — "they," the subject of the promised blessing, as in 5:4, 5:6, 5:7. LSJ confirms oblique-case usage for third person.
- **γῆv (g1093, earth)** is thematically significant — it appears nowhere else in the Beatitudes, making this verse's promise uniquely about earthly (not just heavenly) inheritance. The Strong's gloss "including the occupants in each application" expands the referent from land to world.
- **κlηpovouησou (g2816, inherit)** appears only in this verse in the Beatitudes — no other verse frames its promise as inheritance. This makes 5:5 uniquely about legal bequeathal, not just receiving or having.
