## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed are the meek (Greek πraύς — "mild, gentle," carrying the sense of strength brought to gentleness, as a horse is tamed and bridled; but Jesus was quoting Psalm 37:11, where the Hebrew word `ʻânâv` means "depressed (figuratively), in mind (gentle) or circumstances (needy, especially saintly)" — a dual sense: gentle in spirit AND needy in circumstance, especially in a saintly way. The Greek πraύς captures the "gentle in mind" branch but loses the "needy, especially saintly" branch entirely. The ESV "meek" captures neither — it has drifted toward timidity and submission): for they shall inherit (Greek κλepovouησou — "to be an heir to," a legal term of inheritance and heirship; but the Psalm's Hebrew `yârash` means "to occupy by driving out previous tenants, and possessing in their place" — displacement, seizure, taking possession. The ESV "shall inherit" captures neither the legal bequeathal of the Greek nor the aggressive occupation of the Hebrew) the earth (Greek γῆ — "soil, the whole of the terrene globe, including the occupants in each application"; the Psalm's Hebrew `ʼerets` — "the earth at large, or partitively a land" — matches closely. The ESV captures the referent but loses the inclusiveness — this is the whole inhabited world, not just ground).

**Derivation chain for `ʻânâv` (H6035)** — the Hebrew word behind "meek":
- **H6030** (root): "to eye/heed/pay attention → to respond, speak, testify"
- **H6031** (primitive root): "to depress literally or figuratively → to abase, afflict, humble, weaken, submit"
- **H6035** (the Psalm word): "depressed (figuratively) — in mind (gentle) OR in circumstances (needy, especially saintly)"
- **H6037**: "mildness (royal); also oppressed"
- **H6038**: "condescension — human and subjective (modesty), or divine and objective (clemency)"

The Hebrew word has **two branches**: (1) gentle in mind, and (2) needy in circumstances, *especially saintly*. Jesus's Greek πraύς captures only branch (1). The ESV "meek" captures neither — it has drifted toward timidity.

**Derivation chain for `yârash` (H3423)** — the Hebrew word behind "inherit":
- "a primitive root"
- Core meaning: "to occupy (by driving out previous tenants, and possessing in their place)"
- By implication: "to seize, to rob, to inherit; also to expel, to impoverish, to ruin"

This is NOT passive inheritance. The Hebrew says the meek *occupy* the earth by *driving out* the previous tenants. Psalm 37:9 confirms: "evildoers shall be cut off" — the wicked are removed, and the meek take their place. The Greek κλepovouησou ("to be an heir to") softens this to legal bequeathal. The ESV "shall inherit" loses both.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **Saintly-needy, not weak** (from Hebrew Psalm source): The Hebrew `ʻânâv` (H6035) means "depressed (figuratively), in mind (gentle) or circumstances (needy, especially saintly)." Jesus was blessing those who are *brought low* in circumstance but *gentle* in spirit — *especially in a saintly way*. The Greek πraύς captures only the "gentle in mind" branch. The ESV "meek" has inverted toward weakness. The theological claim Jesus is making — drawing from Psalm 37 — is that the *saintly afflicted* are blessed: those whose circumstances have pressed them down but whose spirit remains gentle and holy. This is the most significant recovery from cross-reference analysis.

2. **Inheritance by displacement, not passive receipt** (from Hebrew Psalm source): The Hebrew `yârash` (H3423) means "to occupy (by driving out previous tenants, and possessing in their place)." Psalm 37:9 says "evildoers shall be cut off" and 37:11 says "the meek shall inherit" — the inheritance is by *displacement* of the wicked. The Greek softens this to legal bequeathal; the ESV loses both. The theological claim is that the meek *take* the earth by displacing the wicked — the earth is vacated by the expelled and occupied by the meek. This is active, not passive.

3. **Jesus calls himself meek** (from NT cross-reference Matthew 11:29): In Matthew 11:29, Jesus says "I am meek and lowly in heart" — using the same ESV word to describe himself. This supports the Hebrew sense of "depressed in circumstances, especially saintly." Jesus is not calling himself weak or timid; he is calling himself *lowly but holy*. The ESV "meek" in Matthew 5:5 should be read through Jesus's own self-description in 11:29: the meek are those who are *lowly in heart* — brought low but still holy.

4. **The dual sense lost in translation** (from Hebrew derivation chain): The Hebrew root H6031 means "to depress → to abase, afflict, humble, weaken, submit." The word H6035 carries both "depressed in mind (gentle)" and "depressed in circumstances (needy, especially saintly)." The derivation also produces H6038: "condescension — human and subjective (modesty), or divine and objective (clemency)." The Hebrew word carries a *spectrum*: from personal modesty to divine clemency. Jesus's Greek πraύvs captures only the personal modesty end. The ESV captures neither.

5. **Parallel with 5:3** (from structural analysis): Verse 5:3 says the poor in spirit possess the kingdom of heaven; 5:5 says the meek inherit the earth. The Hebrew source for both "poor in spirit" and "meek" comes from the same root family (H6031 = "to depress"). Together, they claim that both heavenly and earthly dominion belong to the *depressed* — those brought low in spirit (5:3) and those brought low in circumstance (5:5). The ESV loses this parallel by translating the same Hebrew root family as "poor" in 5:3 and "meek" in 5:5 — two different English words that obscure the shared Hebrew concept of being *brought low*.
