## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are the peacemakers: for they shall be called sons of God.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed are the peacemakers (literally "the ones who build peace" — eἰpynopoió, from the root for building/constructing, suggesting peace is something *made*, not merely achieved): for they shall be called (κλeθ῎σovtaι, "called" in the sense of *named* — given a title of honor) the sons of God.

---

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected. "Peacemakers" is an excellent rendering of eἰpynopoió (g1518), and "shall be called sons of God" faithfully translates κλeθ῎σovtaι (g2564) + uἱoὶ (g5207) + θeoῦ (g2316).

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **eἰpynopoió (g1518)**: "Peacemakers" — from eἰpvoι (peace) + poió (maker/builder). The root poi- means to make or construct. Jesus is saying peace is *built*, not merely wished — it is a constructive act. The ESV's "peacemakers" captures this etymological depth perfectly.
- **uἱoὶ (g5207)**: "Sons/children" — the Strong's gloss is "the children," which is the broader meaning. The ESV's "sons" is the traditional rendering, but the Greek word encompasses both sons and daughters — all children of God.
- **κλeθ῎σovtaι (g2564)**: "Shall be called" — the verb κλeθ- means to call or name. Jesus is saying they will be *given the title* "sons of God" — an act of naming, not merely being.
- **θeoῦ (g2316)**: "Of God" — the genitive of Θe�v (g2316), the same root as 5:8. The sons belong to God.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

Jesus uses the word for *building* (poió) in "peacemakers" — peace is constructed, not stumbled upon. Those who build peace earn a name: "sons of God." The Greek verb κλeθ῎σovtaι (g2564, "shall be called") emphasizes that the title is *bestowed* — God names them as His own. This is not a passive inheritance but an active naming, a divine recognition. The ESV's "peacemakers" preserves the "making" root beautifully — one of the cases where the English etymology matches the Greek.
