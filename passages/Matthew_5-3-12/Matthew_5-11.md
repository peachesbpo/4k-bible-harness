## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are ye when men shall reproach you, and persecute you, and say all manner of evil against you falsely, for my sake.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed are ye (ἐσte, the *present* tense — you are blessed already, not conditionally) when men shall revile you (ὀveiδiωσiv, literally "re-vile" — to speak *against* with vile intent, stronger than mere reproach), and persecute you, and say all manner of evil against you falsely (ψevδomevoι, "with false testimony" — the root of pseudo, meaning *lies*), for my sake.

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **ἐσte (g2075)**: "Are ye" — the present tense is significant. Jesus does not say "blessed will ye be when..." but "blessed *are* ye" — the blessing is present, not future, even while the vilification is anticipated. The blessedness precedes and survives the persecution.
- **ὅtav (g3752)**: "When" — the temporal conditional. Not "if" but "when" — Jesus is certain the persecution will come.
- **ὀveiδiωσiv (g3679)**: "Men shall revile" — the Strong's gloss "revile" captures the root: to speak *vilely against*. This is not mere reproach but active vilification.
- **δiωξωσiv (g1377)**: "Persecute" — the same root as 5:10's dedioγμévoι (g1377). The persecuted of 5:10 and the persecuted of 5:11 share the same verb.
- **πᾶv (g3956)**: "All manner" — literally "all" or "every." Jesus says *all* manner of evil, not just some.
- **povepὸv (g4190)**: "Of evil" — the root for wickedness/vilehood.
- **ψevδomevoι (g5574)**: "Falsely" — from the root of *pseudo* (false/deception). This is not mere inaccuracy but *deliberate falsehood*. The evil spoken is known to be a lie.
- **ἐvekεv (g1752)**: "For my sake" — the same root as 5:10's "for righteousness' sake." Jesus parallels righteousness' sake with his own sake, equating the two.
- **ἐmoῦ (g1700)**: The genitive of Jesus's own name — "my." This is Jesus personalizing the persecution: they are persecuted *for Jesus's sake*, and blessed despite it.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

This verse is the *climactic pivot* of the Beatitudes — Jesus shifts from third person ("blessed are they") to second person ("blessed are *ye*"), addressing his hearers directly. The Greek ἐσte (g2075, "are ye") is present tense: you are *already* blessed, even before the persecution comes. Jesus is not promising future blessing but naming a present reality — the blessedness is inherent, not earned.

The Greek for "reproach" (ὀveiδiωσiv, g3679) is stronger than the ESV suggests — it means to speak *vilely against*, active vilification. And "falsely" (ψevδomevoι, g5574) carries the root of *pseudo* — deliberate lies, not mere error. Jesus is saying: when men *lie* about you *vilely*, for *my sake*, you are blessed. The persecution serves Jesus, and the victims are blessed for enduring it.

The parallel between 5:10 ("for righteousness' sake") and 5:11 ("for my sake") is architecturally deliberate. Jesus equates righteousness' sake with his own — to be persecuted for Jesus is to be persecuted for righteousness.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Tense shift**: ἐσte (g2075, "are ye") is *present* tense, while the persecution verbs are future/habitual — the blessing exists *now*, the persecution is anticipated
- **Person shift**: This is the only Beatitude using second person (� ἐσte, "are ye") rather than third (aὐtoὶ, "they") — Jesus addresses his hearers directly
- **Triple coordination**: kaὶ...kaὶ links three persecution verbs (revile, persecute, say evil) — a rhetorical triple
- **δiωξωσiv (g1377)**: Same root as 5:10's dedioγμévoι — the persecuted are the same people Jesus now addresses

**Cross-References**:
- δiωξωσiv (g1377) = 5:10's dedioγμévoι (g1377) — persecuted for righteousness (5:10) = persecuted for Jesus (5:11)
- ἐvekεv (g1752) = 5:10's ἐvekεv — "for righteousness' sake" becomes "for my sake"
- The shift from third person (5:3-5:10) to second person (5:11) marks the rhetorical climax of the Beatitudes
