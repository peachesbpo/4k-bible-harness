## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets that were before you.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Rejoice (xaïpεte, a singular imperative — Jesus addressing each person directly), and be exceeding glad (ἀgaλλiᾶσthe, the root for *gladness* intensified — not merely happy but *exceedingly* so): for great is your reward in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets that were before you.

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected. "Rejoice" correctly renders xaïpεte (g5463), "exceeding glad" captures ἀgaλλiᾶσthe (g21), and "reward" translates μisθos (g3408) adequately. The ESV's structure preserves the double "for" (ᅁti + γὰp) and the causal chain.

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **xaïpεte (g5463)**: "Rejoice" — from the root for joy/gladden. The imperative singular addresses each hearer directly: *you* rejoice. This is Jesus shifting from "blessed are" (passive) to "rejoice!" (active imperative).
- **ἀgaλλiᾶσthe (g21)**: "Be exceeding glad" — the root ἀgaλλ- relates to *gladness* or *joy*. The ESV's "exceeding glad" captures the intensification — not just glad but *exceedingly* so.
- **μisθos (g3408)**: "Reward" — literally "wages" or "earnings." Jesus uses the language of *compensation* — the reward in heaven is earned, like wages for labor endured.
- **polὺs (g4183)**: "Great" — the same root for *muchness* or *greatness*. The reward is not merely good but *great*.
- **oὐpavoïs (g3772)**: "In heaven" — the same root (oὐpav-) as the "kingdom of heaven" in 5:3 and 5:10. The reward exists *in* heaven — the kingdom where the persecuted are compensated.
- **eδiωxav (g1377)**: "Persecuted they" — the same root as 5:10 and 5:11. The prophets before you were *persecuted* (g1377), just as you are. The persecution is inherited.
- **ppopheτas (g4396)**: "The prophets" — those who came before and were persecuted. Jesus is saying: your suffering is not unique; the prophets endured the same.
- **ppo (g4253)**: "Were before" — the prophets were *before* you. You inherit their persecution and their blessedness.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

This verse is the *closing frame* of the Beatitudes — Jesus shifts from "blessed are" to "rejoice!" The Greek imperative xaïpεte (g5463) is active: not "you are blessed" but "*rejoice!*" — Jesus commands joy as an act, not a state.

The word for "reward" (μisθos, g3408) is the language of *wages* — Jesus is saying heaven *compensates* the persecuted. The reward is earned, like wages for the labor of endurance. This transforms the kingdom of heaven from a spiritual ideal into a *place of compensation* where suffering is acknowledged and repaid.

The closing reference to persecuted prophets (eδiωxav g1377 + ppopheτas g4396) creates a lineage: the prophets before you were persecuted, and so are you — your persecution is inherited, and so is your blessedness. Jesus is saying: you are not alone in your suffering; you carry the prophets' burden, and heaven rewards you as it rewarded them.

The recurrence of oὐpavoïs (g3772, "heaven") from 5:3 and 5:10 completes the frame: the kingdom of heaven (5:3) → claimed by the persecuted (5:10) → where the reward is given (5:12). The Beatitudes are architecturally circular.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Imperative mood**: xaïpεte (g5463) is imperative — Jesus commands, not describes
- **Double causal**: ᅁti (g3754) + γὰp (g1063) — two "for" clauses creating a causal chain: rejoice → because great reward → because prophets persecuted
- **eδiωxav (g1377)**: Same root as 5:10 and 5:11 — the persecuted prophets connect this verse to the persecution theme
- **oὐpavoïs (g3772)**: "Heaven" — same root as "kingdom of heaven" in 5:3 and 5:10, completing the frame

**Cross-References**:
- eδiωxav (g1377, "persecuted") = 5:10's dedioγμévoι and 5:11's δiωξωσiv — the persecution theme unifies 5:10-5:12
- oὐpavoïs (g3772, "heaven") = 5:3 and 5:10's "kingdom of heaven" — the heavenly kingdom is the site of reward
- The shift from passive "blessed are" (5:3-5:10) to imperative "rejoice!" (5:12) marks the closing crescendo of the Beatitudes
- ppopheτas (g4396, "prophets") connects to the broader theme of Matthew 5 as Jesus's sermon to those who would *profess* the truth and suffer for it
