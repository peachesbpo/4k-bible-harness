## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are they that have been persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed are they that have been persecuted (dedioγμévoι, the perfect tense carrying the weight of *continued* persecution — not a single act but an endured condition) for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected. The ESV correctly renders the perfect participle dedioγμévoι (g1377, "have been persecuted"), the causal ἐvekεv (g1752, "for...sake"), and the concluding promise matching 5:3.

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **dedioγμévoι (g1377)**: "Persecuted" — the perfect participle, indicating persecution that has been *endured over time*, not a single event. The ESV's "have been persecuted" captures this perfectly.
- **ἐvekεv (g1752)**: "For the sake of" — a causal/enabling construction. Jesus uses this same root in 5:11 for "for my sake," showing the theological link: righteousness' sake and Jesus's own sake are parallel structures.
- **δikaioσύves (g1343)**: "Righteousness' sake" — the genitive of righteousness. The same root as 5:6's dikaiσùvev (g1343), linking the two verses: those who hunger for righteousness (5:6) and those persecuted for it (5:10) are both blessed.
- **βasiλeia (g932)**: "Kingdom" — the same word as 5:3. The kingdom of heaven bookends the Beatitudes: opened to the poor in spirit (5:3) and claimed by the persecuted (5:10).
- **oὐravῶv (g3772)**: "Of heaven" — the same phrase as 5:3, creating a rhetorical frame.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

This verse is the *pivot* of the Beatitudes — it transforms persecution into qualification. The Greek perfect tense (dedioγμévoι, g1377) emphasizes that persecution is not a single moment but an *endured condition*, and Jesus says: those who endure it *for righteousness' sake* earn the kingdom of heaven. The repetition of βasiλeia (g932) + oὐravῶn (g3772) from 5:3 creates a rhetorical frame: the kingdom opened in 5:3 is claimed in 5:10. The persecuted for righteousness are not pitiable but *honored* — Jesus blesses them because their suffering serves the right.

The connection between 5:6 and 5:10 through dikaiσùves/δikaioσùves (g1343) is architecturally significant: those who *hunger* for righteousness (5:6) and those *persecuted* for it (5:10) are both blessed. Jesus is saying righteousness is worth both desiring and suffering for.
