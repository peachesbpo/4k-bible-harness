## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

Across the 10 Beatitudes, the ESV is notably faithful to the Greek. Only 2 genuine translation diffs were identified:

| # | Verse | Standard (ESV) | What's Lost | Why It Matters |
|---|-------|----------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 5:5 | "the meek" | Greek πraύς (g4239) literally means **strength under control** — from πra- (forth) + aύς (strength). The same word used for a horse that has been *tamed and bridled*. The ESV's "meek" has drifted toward weakness/timidity — nearly the opposite of the Greek. | Jesus blesses those who *have* power but keep it *reined in* — the bridled, not the weak. The earth goes to the controlled, not the feeble. |
| 2 | 5:11 | "reproach" | Greek ὀveiδiωσiv (g3679) means "to speak *vilely against*" — active vilification, not mild disapproval | Jesus describes hostile, vile speech against the person, not gentle criticism |
| 3 | 5:11 | "falsely" | Greek ψevδomevoι (g5574) carries the root of *pseudo* — deliberate lies/false testimony, not mere inaccuracy | The evil spoken is *known falsehood* — the accusers are liars, not mistaken |

**Verses with no significant translation loss**: 5:3, 5:4, 5:6, 5:7, 5:8, 5:9, 5:10, 5:12 — the ESV faithfully renders the Greek meaning across these verses, though etymological richness is documented in each verse file.

## ✨ Expanded Translation (4K)

Blessed are the poor in spirit (literally "the wretched ones" — πtwxos carries the sense of brokenness, not merely poverty): for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted (literally "called beside" — comfort as companionship, not consolation).

Blessed are the meek (literally "the bridled ones" — πraύς, from πra- + aύς, meaning *strength under control*, like a horse that has been tamed and bridled: not weakness, but power governed): for they shall inherit the earth.

Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled (the same root as filling a vessel — satisfaction as fullness, not merely contentment).

Blessed are the merciful: for they shall obtain mercy (the same root in both — mercy as its own reward, the giver receives what they give).

Blessed are the pure in heart (literally "the clean ones" — absence of contamination, not merely moral goodness): for they shall see God.

Blessed are the peacemakers (literally "the ones who build peace" — peace as construction, not passive absence of conflict): for they shall be called sons of God.

Blessed are they that have been persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

Blessed are ye when men shall revile you (to speak vilely against — not mere reproach but active vilification), and persecute you, and say all manner of evil against you falsely (with false testimony — deliberate lies, not mere inaccuracy), for my sake.

Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward (literally "wages" — compensation earned, like wages for labor) in heaven: for so persecuted they the prophets that were before you.

## 💡 Theological Insights

1. **The Kingdom Frame (5:3 ↔ 5:10)**: βasiλeia (g932, "kingdom") + oὐpavῶn (g3772, "of heaven") appears in both 5:3 and 5:10, creating a rhetorical frame. The kingdom opened to the poor in spirit is claimed by the persecuted for righteousness.

2. **The Persecution Lineage (5:10-5:12)**: The same root for persecution (g1377) links 5:10 (persecuted for righteousness), 5:11 (persecute for Jesus's sake), and 5:12 (prophets persecuted before you). Jesus builds a lineage of suffering that is also a lineage of blessedness.

3. **Righteousness and Jesus Equated (5:10 ↔ 5:11)**: ἐvekεv (g1752, "for...sake") appears in 5:10 as "for righteousness' sake" and 5:11 as "for my sake." Jesus parallels the two, equating righteousness with himself.

4. **Bridged Strength, Not Weakness (5:5)**: Greek πraύς (g4239) literally means strength under control — the same word used for a horse that has been tamed and bridled. Jesus is not blessing the weak but the *governed* — those who have power and keep it reined in. The ESV's "meek" has drifted toward timidity, inverting the Greek sense.

5. **Mercy as Its Own Reward (5:7)": The same root in ἐleηmovés (merciful) and ἐleηθ῎σovtaι (shall obtain mercy) — the giver receives what they give. Mercy is its own compensation.

6. **Peace as Construction (5:9)": The root for building (poió) in eἰpynopoió ("peacemakers") — peace is *made*, not merely achieved. The ESV's "peacemakers" preserves this etymology.

7. **Present Tense Blessing (5:11)": The Greek ἐσte (g2075, "are ye") is present tense — the blessing exists *now*, even before persecution comes. Blessedness is inherent, not earned.

8. **Wages in Heaven (5:12)": μisθos (g3408, "reward") is the language of wages/compensation. Heaven *pays* the persecuted for their endurance.

9. **The Imperative Shift (5:12)": Jesus shifts from passive "blessed are" (5:3-5:10) to imperative "rejoice!" (5:12) — commanding joy as an act, not a state.

## 📝 Notes for Further Study

- **Hebrew/OT echoes**: The Beatitudes echo Jesus's earlier sermon (Matthew 3) where he introduces "blessed" language for the tempted. Hebrew analysis could reveal if the OT uses the same root.
- **πtwxos (g4434, "poor") deeper range**: The root carries connotations of brokenness and wretchedness beyond mere poverty — worth a full lexical range study.
- **The "sons of God" formula (5:9)**: uἱoὶ (g5207) means "children" more broadly than "sons" — does this matter for Jesus's theology of gender and divine parentage?
- **Rhetorical structure**: The Beatitudes follow a consistent anaphoric pattern (Μaκapiou + subject + ᅁti + promise) that would reward full structural analysis across all 10 verses.
