> Blessed are they that mourn: for they shall be comforted.

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed (graced by God, honored) are they that mourn (who grieve, who lament, who carry sorrow): for they shall be comforted (consoled, companioned in their grief, called alongside to find rest).

---

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected.

**Explanation**: The ESV faithfully conveys the Greek:
- "Blessed" renders *μακάριοι* accurately (same as all Beatitudes)
- "they that mourn" faithfully renders *οἱ πενθοῦντες* (the mourning ones / those who grieve)
- "for they shall be comforted" faithfully renders *ὅti αὐτοὶ παρακληθήσονται* (because they shall be consoled)

The ESV's "mourn" captures the Greek *πενθοῦντες* well — both convey grief and sorrow. The ESV's "comforted" captures *παρακληθονταu* — both convey consolation and relief from grief.

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **πενθοῦνtes** (*pentheoutes*): From *πen-* (about, around) + *θe* (to put, place) → "to place around" → "to mourn, grieve, lament." The word conveys active, ongoing grief — not a state but a process. The participial form (-ontes) means "the mourning ones," those who are in the act of grieving.
- **παρακλeθσontaI** (*paraklethsond*): From *para-* (beside, alongside) + *kλeθ-* (call, cry) + *-son* (verbal suffix) → "to call beside" → "to comfort, console, give consolation." The etymology reveals the nature of comfort: it is not merely alleviation but companionship — being called alongside someone in their distress. The passive/middle form (*-ontai*) means "shall be comforted" — they shall receive consolation.
- **αὐtoὶ** (*autoi*): Third-person plural pronoun "they" — the same people who mourn are the ones who shall be comforted. The ESV preserves this identity of subject across both clauses.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

- **Grief leads to consolation**: The verse promises that mourning is not a permanent state — those who grieve shall be comforted. The theological claim is that God's comfort awaits those who suffer.
- **Comfort as companionship**: The etymology of *παρακλeθσon* (to call beside) reveals that "comfort" in the Greek is not merely relief from pain but companionship in suffering — being called alongside someone. This theological comfort is relational, not just palliative.
- **The parallel of mourning and comforting**: Both *πenθeoutes* (mourning) and *παρακλeθσontaI* (being comforted) are participial forms — they describe ongoing processes, not static states. The ESV captures this with "that mourn" and "shall be comforted," preserving the dynamic quality.
- **The same "they"**: The Greek uses *αὐtoὶ* (they) to link the two clauses — the mourners and the comforted are the same people. This theological identity is preserved in the ESV.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- *πenθeoutes* is a present participle in the nominative plural — "the mourning ones." It functions substantivally: "those who are mourning."
- *ὅti* is a causal conjunction meaning "because, for" — it introduces the explanatory/result clause.
- *αὐtoὶ* is the third-person plural pronoun "they," referring back to the mourners.
- *παρακλeθσontaI* is in the passive/middle voice with the future sense conveyed by the *-on-* stem and *-tai* ending — "shall be comforted." The future sense is important: the comfort is promised, not yet given.
- The verse structure parallels 5:3 exactly: [Blessed] + [those with a condition] + [for they shall receive a blessing].

**Cross-References**:
- *Μakapos* (g3107) — the signature Beatitude word, appears in every verse.
- *ὅti* (g3754) — the causal conjunction appears in every Beatitude, always introducing the promised blessing.
- *αὐtoὶ* (g846) — "they" — appears in 5:4, 5:5, 5:6, 5:7, and 5:8, always as the subject of the promised blessing.
- *παρακλeθσon* (comfort/consolation) is thematically related to the overall Beatitude structure of suffering followed by divine blessing.
