## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **καθapὶ (g2513)**: "Pure/clean" — the root kaθap- relates to clarity and cleanness. Jesus chooses a word that implies *absence of contamination*, not merely moral goodness. The pure heart is one that has not been stained or corrupted.
- **kapδiᾳ (g2588)**: "Heart" — the same root that gives us "cardiac" in medical contexts. Jesus means the *innermost* core of the person, not just emotional feeling.
- **Θe�ov (g2316)**: "God" — the accusative form of theos. Jesus uses the definite article (τὸv Θe�ov, "the God"), emphasizing a specific, particular God — not an abstract deity but *the* divine one.
- **ὄψovtaι (g3700)**: "Shall see/behold" — the future tense carries a sense of promised beholding. This is not seeing as casual looking, but seeing as *perceiving the face* of God, which is thematically central to Jesus's theology of vision.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

The purity Jesus names is not abstract moral virtue but *cleanness* — the absence of contamination. The Greek root for "pure" (kaθap-) is the same used for clean water or clean hands. Jesus is saying: the undefiled heart will *see* God, where seeing means perceiving the divine face. This connects to Jesus's broader theology that God is veiled by impurity — the stained heart cannot see, but the clean one can behold. The promise is future ("shall see"), not present — purity earns the vision over time.
