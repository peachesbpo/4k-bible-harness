## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed (graced by God, honored) are the poor (wretched, humble, lacking in material abundance) in spirit: for theirs (belonging to them, of their own) is the kingdom (the realm, the dominion of the sovereign) of heaven.

---

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the core meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected.

**Explanation**: The ESV conveys every essential element of the Greek:
- "Blessed" faithfully renders *μακάριοι* (blessed, made happy/fortunate)
- "poor in spirit" faithfully renders *πτωχοὶ τῷ πνευματι* (poor/wretched in the spirit)
- "for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" faithfully renders *ὅti αὐτῶν ἐσtiν ἡ βασiλeia τῶn οὐraνῶn*

The one nuance worth noting — the Greek *πτωχοὶ* carries connotations of "wretched, miserable" beyond the more clinical English "poor" — is better classified as an etymological observation than a true translation loss, since "poor" is the standard and accepted rendering of this Greek term in theological contexts.

---

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **Μακάριοι** (*makarios*): From the root *mak-* (glory, honor) + *-arios* (adjective suffix). The word means "blessed, happy, fortunate, content." It is the standard term used throughout the Beatitudes for "Blessed" — a formal, elevated usage that conveys not just happiness but being graced or honored by God.
- **πτωχοὶ** (*ptōchos*): From *πtw-* (few, little) + *-oxos* (having, possessing). Literally "having little" → "poor, wretched, miserable." The word carries emotional weight beyond mere material lack — it conveys suffering and humility. Related to *πtwis* (few) and *πtwiox* (poverty).
- **πνευματι** (*pneumati*): Dative of *πneuma* (spirit, breath). The root *pneu-* relates to breathing/spirit, cognate with "pneumatic" in English (from Greek). The dative case here means "in [the] spirit" — spiritual poverty, not material poverty.
- **βασiλeia** (*basileia*): From *basile* (king, founder, sovereign) + *-eia* (abstract noun suffix). Literally "the realm of the king" → "kingdom." The word carries royal/dominial connotations — this is not just any place, but the sovereign realm.
- **οὐraνῶn** (*ouranōn*): Genitive of *oupavos* (heaven, the heavens). The word can mean both the physical heavens (sky) and the spiritual divine realm. In this context, it means the spiritual/divine heaven.

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

- **Spiritual, not material, poverty**: The Greek makes clear through the dative *πνευmatι* (in spirit) that this is about spiritual humility, not material destitution. The ESV captures this with "in spirit," preserving the crucial distinction.
- **The kingdom belongs to the humble**: The construction *αὐtῶn ἐσtiν ἡ βasiλeia* (theirs is the kingdom) makes a radical theological claim — the kingdom of heaven does not belong to the powerful or wealthy, but to those who are humble in spirit. The ESV preserves this possessive construction faithfully.
- **"Blessed" as theological reversal**: The use of *Makarios* (blessed) for those who are "poor" (wretched, humble) is itself the theological statement — poverty of spirit is not a curse but a blessing. This rhetorical structure is the heart of the Beatitudes.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- The verse uses a copular construction: *Makapos* (blessed) is a predicative adjective modifying *ptwixois* (the poor), with the copula implied in the participial form.
- *πneumatι* is in the dative case, indicating "in [the] spirit" — a prepositional phrase modifying the adjective *ptwixois*.
- *ὅti* is a causal conjunction meaning "because, for" — it introduces the explanatory clause.
- *αὐtῶn* is a possessive pronoun (genitive of *αὐtos* = "of them") meaning "theirs."
- *ἐσtiν* is the third-person singular present verb "is."
- *βasiλeia* is in the nominative case as the subject of *ἐσtiν*.
- *οὐraνῶn* is in the genitive case, dependent on *βasiλeia* — "kingdom [of] heaven."

**Cross-References**:
- *Makapos* (g3107) appears in every Beatitude — it is the signature word of the series.
- *βasiλeia* (g932) reappears in Matthew 5:10 with the same construction ("theirs is the kingdom of heaven").
- *πneuma* (spirit, g4151) connects thematically to Matthew 5:8 (*kaρdian* = heart) — both verses describe an internal quality of character.
