## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

Blessed (graced by God, honored) are they that hunger (yearn, ache with desire) and thirst (crave, long intensely) after righteousness (justice, upright conduct, moral rectitude): for they shall be filled (satisfied, satiated, their longing made full).

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

The ESV accurately captures the meaning of this verse. No significant translation loss detected.

**Explanation**: The ESV faithfully conveys every element of the Greek:
- "they that hunger" renders *πeiνondeS* — from *πeiν-* (hunger, yearn) — the ESV "hunger" is accurate
- "and thirst" renders *kaὶ diψondeS* — from *diψ-* (thirst, crave) — the ESV "thirst" is accurate
- "after righteousness" renders *twn dikaiσuneν* — *dikaiσuve* = righteousness, justice — the ESV "righteousness" is accurate
- "they shall be filled" renders *aὐtoὶ xorTasthσondai* — *xorTasthson* = to fill, satiate — the ESV "filled" is accurate

A subtle nuance: the Greek *xorTasthson* carries the sense of "satiate, satisfy fully" — not merely "fill" but "make full to the point of satisfaction." The ESV "filled" captures the primary meaning, though "satisfied" or "satiate" would convey the nuance of desire fulfillment more precisely. This is an etymological observation rather than a true translation loss, as "filled" is the standard theological rendering and conveys the essential meaning.

## 📝 Etymological Notes (commentary, not translation loss)

- **πeiνondeS** (*peiontes*): From *πein-* (hunger, yearn, desire) + *-ondeS* (participial suffix). The word conveys active, ongoing hunger — not just wanting but yearning, aching with desire. The participial form means "the hungering ones."
- **diψondeS** (*dipsondes*): From *diψs-* (thirst, crave) + *-ondeS* (participial suffix). The word conveys thirst as craving — an intense, visceral longing. The use of two distinct verbs of desire (*πein-* and *diψ-*) emphasizes the depth and duality of the longing.
- **dikaiσuneν** (*dikaisunev*): From *dikai-* (right, straight, upright) + *σuve* (quality, -ness) → "righteousness, justice, upright conduct, rectitude." The word carries moral weight — this is not just any desire but a hunger for justice and moral rectitude.
- **xorTasthσondai** (*xortasthsondai*): From *xorT-* (fill, satiate, satisfy) + *-asthσon* (verbal suffix) + *-dai* (passive/future ending). The word means "shall be filled, shall be satiated" — the future passive conveys both promise and fulfillment. The satiation sense is important: this is not just being filled but being satisfied to the point of having the desire resolved.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

- **Dual desire for righteousness**: The Greek uses two distinct verbs of desire — *πeinthe* (hunger) and *diψthe* (thirst) — emphasizing the intensity and duality of the longing for righteousness. The ESV preserves this with "hunger and thirst," capturing the theological claim that righteousness is worth yearning for with every fiber of desire.
- **Righteousness as the object of longing**: *dikaiσuve* (righteousness, justice) is the object of both hunger and thirst — the verse claims that righteousness is not a burden but a craving, something the soul yearns for as the body hungers and thirsts.
- **Satiation, not just filling**: The Greek *xorTasthson* carries the sense of satiation — the hungering and thirsting for righteousness shall be fully satisfied, not merely partially addressed. The theological claim is that righteousness fulfills the soul's deepest longings completely.
- **The parallel of desire and fulfillment**: The structure mirrors the theological movement: those who ache with desire for justice shall have that ache fully resolved. The ESV captures this parallel with "hunger and thirst" → "shall be filled."

**Grammatical Observations**:
- *πeiνondeS* and *diψondeS* are both present participles in the nominative plural — "the hungering ones" and "the thirsting ones." They are coordinated by *kaὶ* (and), conveying dual desire.
- *twn dikaiσuneν* is in the genitive/accusative — "after righteousness" or "for righteousness." The Greek uses the genitive to indicate the object of the hungering and thirsting.
- *xorTasthσondai* is in the passive/middle voice with future sense — "shall be filled." The *-on-* stem and *-dai* ending convey both futurity and passivity: this is a promise that fulfillment will come.
- The verse follows the standard Beatitude structure: [Blessed] + [those with a quality] + [for they shall receive a blessing].

**Cross-References**:
- *Μakapos* (g3107) — the signature Beatitude word.
- *dikaiσuve* (g1343, righteousness) reappears in Matthew 5:10 (*dikaiσuneS* — righteousness' sake), linking these two verses thematically.
- *kaὶ* (g2532, and) — the conjunction also appears in 5:11 and 5:12.
- *xorTasthson* (g5526, to fill/satiate) appears only here in the Beatitudes — the concept of being "filled" is unique to this verse.
