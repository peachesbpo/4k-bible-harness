## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Thou blind Pharisee, cleanse first the inside of the cup and of the platter, that the outside thereof may become clean also.

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Thou blind (and "blind" means opaque, as if smoky — clouded vision of the mind and judgment, not just the eyes) Pharisee (and "Pharisee" means a separatist — one who has made himself exclusively religious, a sect apart), cleanse (and "cleanse" is a purification verb — the same word used for cleansing from leprosy, cleansing the conscience, purifying from stains; its semantic category is "purity rite," not just "wash") first (and "first" means firstly in time, place, order, OR importance — the inside must be cleansed first because it is the foundation) the inside (and "the inside" means that which is within — the interior, the same word used in v25 for where the cup was full from sin) of the cup (and "cup" means a drinking-vessel, but by extension a cupful or draught, and figuratively a LOT or FATE — the cup is a metaphor for one's portion in life) and of the platter (and "platter" means a side-dish — the same receptacle from v25, which the classical lexicon shows used metaphorically for "fresh tastes of misery"), that (and "that" is ἵna — "in order that," denoting the PURPOSE or the RESULT; the inside cleansing is the MEANS, the outside cleanness is the END) the outside (and "the outside" means the exterior — the same surface the Pharisees were already cleansing in v25) thereof (and "thereof" is the reflexive — of the cup and platter themselves; the outside belongs to the same vessels whose inside must be cleansed) may become (and "may become" is the middle voice — "to cause to be, to come into being"; the outside doesn't just "be" clean — it BECOMES clean through the inside cleansing; the becoming is conditional, not automatic) clean (and "clean" is the adjective form of the same purification root — and its semantic categories include "righteousness/holiness" and "purity rite"; the outside doesn't just become spotless, it becomes RIGHTEOUS, HOLY, PURE) also (and "also" means "and too" — the outside may become clean AS WELL AS the inside; both clean, inside first).

### Concepts Flattened

- **The reversal reduced to word order**: V25 says "cleanse the outside... but within they are full from sin." V26 says "cleanse first the inside... that the outside may become clean also." The REVERSAL — outside-first to inside-first — IS the theology. ESV captures the word order but loses the force of the reversal: Jesus is correcting the Pharisees' entire approach.
- **Purpose clause reduced to "that"**: The Greek ἵna means "in order that" — a purposive conjunction denoting the PURPOSE or RESULT. The inside cleansing is the MEANS; the outside cleanness is the END. ESV's "that" captures the conjunction but loses the purposive force: the outside doesn't just happen to become clean — it becomes clean BECAUSE the inside was cleansed first.
- **"May become" reduced to "may become"**: The Greek gιnomai (middle voice, "to cause to be, to come into being") is a dynamic becoming — not a static being. The outside BECOMES clean through the inside cleansing. The middle voice suggests generation: the inside cleansing GENERATES the outside cleanness. ESV's "may become" captures the form but loses the generative force.
- **"Clean" as moral purity reduced to physical cleanness**: The Greek kaθaρos has categories `morality_spirituality>righteousness_holiness` and `ritual>purity_rite`. The outside becoming "clean" means becoming RIGHTEOUS, HOLY, PURE — not just spotless. ESV's "clean" captures the surface sense but loses the moral depth.
- **Singular "thou" vs. plural "you"**: V25 addresses "you" (plural: scribes and Pharisees and hypocrites). V26 addresses "thou" (singular: one blind Pharisee). Jesus narrows from the group to the individual. The command is now personal — directed at one person. ESV captures the singular "thou" but doesn't mark the shift from group to individual.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The reversal IS the theology**: V25 condemns: you cleanse the OUTSIDE while the INSIDE is full from robbery and incontinence. V26 commands: cleanse the INSIDE FIRST. Jesus doesn't just add a new indictment — he REVERSES the Pharisees' entire approach. The order reversal (outside-first → inside-first) IS the theological correction. You cannot have purity without substance. The surface without the interior is not purity but performance. Jesus says: start from the inside. The outside will follow.

2. **"First" as importance, not just sequence**: The Greek πρῶton means "firstly in time, place, order, OR importance." The Strong's definition explicitly includes importance. Jesus isn't just saying "do this first and then do the outside." He's saying the inside is the FIRST THING — the most important thing. The inside is the FOUNDATION. Without a clean inside, a clean outside is meaningless. The priority IS the theology: substance before surface, reality before appearance, interior before exterior.

3. **The purpose clause: inside cleansing GENERATES outside cleanness**: The Greek ἵna ("in order that") introduces a purpose clause. The inside cleansing is the MEANS; the outside cleanness is the END. Jesus doesn't say "cleanse the inside and also the outside" (two tasks). He says "cleanse the inside IN ORDER THAT the outside may become clean" (one task, one consequence). The grammar encodes causal dependence: the outside cleanness DEPENDS ON the inside cleansing. You don't get a clean outside by cleansing the outside. You get a clean outside by cleansing the inside FIRST. The outside cleanness is a CONSEQUENCE of the inside cleanness, not a separate task.

4. **"May become" as conditional generation**: The Greek gιnomai (middle voice, "to cause to be, to come into being") is dynamic — a becoming, not a being. The outside doesn't automatically become clean; it MAY become clean — if the inside is cleansed first. The middle voice suggests generation: the inside cleansing GENERATES the outside cleanness. The becoming is conditional, not guaranteed. It requires the inside work. Jesus is saying: if you do the inside work, the outside will BECOME clean — it will come into being as clean, generated by the inside cleansing.

5. **"Clean" as righteous and holy**: The Greek kaθaρos (clean) has semantic categories `morality_spirituality>righteousness_holiness` and `ritual>purity_rite`. The outside doesn't just become spotless — it becomes RIGHTEOUS, HOLY, PURE. Jesus is saying: if you cleanse the inside first, the outside will become TRULY holy — not just apparently clean. The cleanness is moral, spiritual, theological. The Pharisees' outside cleanness (v25) was a performance; the cleanness Jesus promises (v26) is real. The same word — kaθaρos — spans physical cleanness and moral holiness, and Jesus uses it to say: real cleanness IS holiness.

6. **Singular "thou": from group to individual**: V25 addresses "you" (plural: scribes and Pharisees and hypocrites). V26 addresses "thou" (singular: one blind Pharisee). Jesus narrows from the group to the individual. The command is now personal — directed at one person. The shift makes the command more intimate and more challenging: this is not about "those people" anymore; this is about YOU, the one Pharisee who is blind. Jesus looks at one Pharisee and says: cleanse your own inside first. The singular makes it a personal charge, not a general statement.

7. **The same vessels: cup and platter as continuity**: Jesus uses the exact same words for "cup" (poth̄rion) and "platter" (paρoψis) as in v25. The continuity is deliberate: v25 describes the problem (outside clean, inside full from sin), v26 provides the remedy (cleanse the inside first). The cup is still a metaphor for one's LOT or FATE. The platter still dispenses "fresh tastes of misery." Jesus is saying: take the same vessels you were already cleansing — but start from the other side. The vessels don't change; the approach does.

8. **"Also" as the goal: both clean, inside first**: The Greek kaί ("also, too") encodes the goal: the outside may become clean ALSO — as well as the inside. Both clean. But the order matters: inside first, outside ALSO. The "also" means the cleanness SPREADS from inside to outside. V25's outside-only cleanness was not real cleanness; it was performance. V26's inside-first cleanness generates outside cleanness ALSO — and that outside cleanness is now REAL (because it comes from the inside). The "also" is the promise: if you do the inside work, you get the outside cleanness too — but as a CONSEQUENCE, not as a performance.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Imperative mood (kaθaɾιson)**: The verb is in the imperative — a direct command. Jesus isn't describing or suggesting; he is COMMANDING. "Cleanse first the inside!" The imperative makes v26 a direct order, contrasting with v25's indicative ("ye cleanse the outside"). V25 describes what they do; v26 commands what they should do.
- **Singular "thou" (imperative 2nd singular)**: The imperative kaθaɾιson is 2nd singular — addressed to ONE Pharisee. V25 used kaθaɾιzeτe (2nd plural) — addressed to the group. Jesus narrows from the group to the individual. The command is personal.
- **Purpose clause with ἵna + optative**: The conjunction ἵna ("in order that") introduces a purpose clause with the verb in the optative mood (γέneσai — "may become"). The optative expresses what MAY or SHOULD happen as a result. The inside cleansing is the means; the outside cleanness is the intended result. The grammar encodes causal dependence: the outside cleanness depends on the inside cleansing.
- **Middle voice (γέneσai from gιnomai)**: The verb gιnomai is "a prolongation and middle voice form of a primary verb." The middle voice is reflexive — the subject both acts and is affected. The outside doesn't just "be" clean (static); it "becomes" clean (dynamic, generated). The becoming is the RESULT of the inside cleansing.
- **Adverbial πρῶton (first)**: The adverb πρῶton modifies the imperative verb — "cleanse FIRST." The Strong's definition includes "in time, place, order, or importance." The adverb positions the inside cleansing as the FIRST action — not just first in sequence but first in priority.
- **Adjective kaθaρos (clean) with moral categories**: The adjective kaθaρos has semantic categories `morality_spirituality>righteousness_holiness` and `ritual>purity_rite`. The outside becoming "clean" means becoming RIGHTEOUS, HOLY, PURE. The cleanness is moral, not just physical.
- **Etymology of kaθaρos (clean)**: The LSJ shows kaθaρos used for physical cleanness (spotless), moral purity (free from guilt, undefiled), ceremonial purity (pure from all pollution), and moral character (clean hands = honesty). Jesus uses the word that spans all these senses: the outside becoming "clean" means becoming clean in every sense — physically, morally, spiritually.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- This verse is the DIRECT RESPONSE to v25. V25 describes the problem: you cleanse the outside while the inside is full from robbery and incontinence. V26 provides the remedy: cleanse the inside first, that the outside may become clean also. The two verses form a pair — problem and solution.
- The cup and platter are the exact same words as v25. The vessels don't change; the approach does. Jesus is saying: take the same vessels you were already cleansing — but start from the other side.
- The singular "thou" (one Pharisee) contrasts with v25's plural "you" (scribes and Pharisees and hypocrites). Jesus narrows from the group to the individual. The command is now personal — directed at one blind Pharisee.
- The "blind" epithet carries over from v24 (blind guides). The Pharisee is still blind — but now Jesus is speaking to that blindness directly, commanding the Pharisee to cleanse what he cannot see (the inside).
- This verse connects to Jesus' broader theme throughout Matthew 23 of the difference between external conformity and internal reality. The Pharisees perform the outside of purity (v25) while harboring the inside of sin. Jesus says: start from the inside. The outside will follow. The theology is: real purity begins within.

**Cross-References**:
- **Matthew 23:25** (preceding verse) — "Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye cleanse the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full from extortion and excess." V25 describes the problem; v26 provides the remedy. Same vessels, reversed order.
- **Matthew 23:24** (preceding woe) — "Ye blind guides, that strain out the gnat, and swallow the camel!" The "blind" epithet carries from v24 to v26. The blindness is now personal — directed at one Pharisee.
- **Matthew 23:3** (hypocrites) — "Woe unto you... hypocrites!" The theatrical metaphor (stage-players performing a role) finds its spatial expression in v25 (outside vs. inside) and its remedy in v26 (cleanse the inside first). The actor performs the outside; Jesus says: start from the inside.
- **Matthew 8:2** (cleanse from leprosy) — The LSJ shows kaθaɾιze used in Ev.Matt.8.2 for cleansing from leprosy. Jesus uses the same purification verb in v26 — commanding the Pharisee to perform the RITE of purification, starting from the inside. The connection to leprosy (a disease) suggests that the inside of the cup is not just dirty but DISEASED — and the cure must start from within.
