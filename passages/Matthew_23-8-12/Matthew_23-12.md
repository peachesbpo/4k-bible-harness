## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

For whoever exalts himself (you lift yourself up by your own choice) will be humbled (God will bring you low — with certainty, as a divine law), and whoever humbles himself (you lower yourself by your own choice — emptying self-reliance to receive God's fullness) will be exalted (God will lift you up — with the same certainty). The choice is yours; God's response is certain: God reverses whatever you do to yourself.

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The active/passive LAW — God reverses what you do to yourself**: The grammatical structure of v12 encodes Jesus' deepest theological teaching: Whoever exalts HIMSELF (active — you do it to yourself) → will be HUMBLED (passive — God does it to you). Whoever humbles HIMSELF (active — you do it to yourself) → will be EXALTED (passive — God does it to you). The active voice marks YOUR choice; the passive voice marks GOD'S response. God REVERSES what you do to yourself. The future indicative tense makes this a CERTAINTY — a divine law, not a guideline.

2. **tapeinos — humility as spiritual dependence on God**: BibleHub's Helps Word-studies on tapeinoō (G5013): "With the believer, tapeinoō ('show humility, true lowliness') happens by being fully dependent on the Lord — dismissing reliance upon self (self-governments) and emptying carnal ego. This exalts the Lord as our all-in-all and prompts the gift of His fullness in us." Humbling oneself is not self-abasement but SPIRITUAL DEPENDENCE on God — emptying the self to receive God's fullness. The one who does this, God exalts. The one who refuses, God humbles.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Active vs. Passive contrast — the theological heart**: The grammatical structure of v12 encodes Jesus' theological teaching with precision:
  - "Whoever exalts HIMSELF" (active — self does it) → "will be HUMBLED" (passive — God does it)
  - "Whoever humbles HIMSELF" (active — self does it) → "will be EXALTED" (passive — God does it)
  
  The ACTIVE voice marks what YOU do to yourself (exalt or humble). The PASSIVE voice marks what GOD does to you (humble or exalt). Jesus' teaching: if you lift yourself up, God will bring you down. If you bring yourself down, God will lift you up. The active/passive contrast IS the theology — God REVERSES what you do to yourself. ESV preserves this contrast ("exalts himself" / "will be humbled" / "humbles himself" / "will be exalted") but does not make the active/passive voices explicit as theological claims.
- **Future indicative (all four verbs)**: All four verbs are FUTURE INDICATIVE — not subjunctive (might), not optative (should), but INDICATIVE (will). This is not a wish or a moral guideline but a DECLARATION OF FACT. Jesus states what WILL happen as certainly as a law of nature: pride WILL be humbled, humility WILL be exalted. The future indicative is the tense of DIVINE CERTAINTY.
- **�pso (G5311) and tapeinos (G5011) — height and lowness**: The two roots are EXACT OPPOSITES: �pso = "height, high" and tapeinos = "lowness, lowly." �psoō = "to lift up on high" (exalt) and tapeinoō = "to make low" (humble). The verse pairs them as mirror images: exalt/humble, humble/exalt. The theological insight is that height and lowness are God's to bestow or remove — you may position yourself (active), but God sets the final altitude (passive).
- **heauton (G1438) — "himself" reflexive**: The reflexive pronoun appears in BOTH halves: whoever exalts HIMSELF, whoever humbles HIMSELF. The point is that the ACTION is on the self — it's not done TO you by others but BY you TO you. YOU choose to exalt or humble yourself. God's response (passive) is determined by YOUR choice (active reflexive). The theological weight: your destiny is in YOUR hands — humble yourself, and God will exalt you.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- **The closing principle**: Verse 12 is the CLOSING PRINCIPLE of the entire "Seven Woes" pericope (23:1-12). Jesus has exposed the Pharisees' pride (vv1-7), prohibited earthly titles (vv8-10), commanded servanthood (v11), and now declares the LAW: pride will be humbled, humility will be exalted. This is the divine principle that governs all the preceding verses — the Pharisees exalt themselves and will be humbled; the disciples who humble themselves will be exalted.
- **�psoō and the Passion**: BibleHub's Thayer lexicon notes that �psoō (to exalt) is used of Jesus' own exaltation through the cross: "the lifting up includes death and the victory over death; the passion itself is regarded as a glorification." Jesus humbled himself through death on the cross (Philippians 2:5-8) and God exalted him to the highest place (Philippians 2:9-11). Jesus' own life enacts the principle of v12: he humbled himself, and God exalted him. The Pharisees do the reverse — they exalt themselves, and God will humble them.
- **tapeinos and the humble life**: BibleHub's Helps Word-studies: "With the believer, tapeinoō ("show humility, true lowliness") happens by being fully dependent on the Lord — dismissing reliance upon self (self-governments) and emptying carnal ego. This exalts the Lord as our all-in-all and prompts the gift of His fullness in us." Humbling oneself is not self-abasement but SPIRITUAL DEPENDENCE on God — emptying the self to receive God's fullness. The one who does this God exalts; the one who refuses God humbles.
