## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> The greatest among you shall be your servant.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

And the greatest among you (the one who surpasses all others — the most gifted, the most capable) shall be your servant (and "servant" means minister, deacon — one who serves others, who carries out the Lord's plan for his people; even the greatest must dwell as one who serves, not one who is served).

## 📊 Diff Analysis: What Got Lost?

| # | Standard (ESV) | What's Lost | Why It Matters |

| 1 | "will be" (v11) | The future tense marks *certainty* — ordained, not suggested. The "middle voice" means the greatest will not just serve but will *BE* a servant. | Jesus declares a future fact: the greatest among you will dwell as a servant. It's who they ARE, not just what they do. |
| 2 | "servant" | The word means servant, minister, AND *deacon* (it's the root of the English word "deacon"). | The full semantic range: one who serves, who ministers, who carries out the Lord's plan for his people. |

---

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **The servant-deacon — greatness IS servanthood**: diakonos (G1249) carries the full range of servant, minister, AND deacon. The root of the English "deacon" — a serving one in the NT. Jesus' command in v11 that the greatest among the disciples shall be a SERVANT means: true greatness IS servanthood. In God's kingdom, the greatest person is not the one who is served by others but the one who SERVES others. Jesus himself modeled this (John 13:14-15, washing disciples' feet; Mark 10:45, "the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve").

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **Future indicative middle (estai, G1510)**: The future tense marks CERTAINTY — not possibility or obligation but certainty. "The greatest among you WILL BE your servant." Jesus declares this as a future fact, ordained by God. The middle voice (Greek's distinctive voice between active and passive) means the subject both ACTS and IS ACTED UPON — the greatest will serve and will exist in the role of servant. It's not just what he does but WHAT HE IS.
- **meizon (G3173) — the comparative/superlative "greatest"**: Not megas (great) but meizon (greater/greatest) — the comparative form. Jesus addresses not just the good or the capable but the GREATEST — the superlative, the one who surpasses all. Even the MOST great among you — the one who might seem to deserve a title — even THAT one shall be a servant. The superlative makes the point absolute: no one is exempt.
- **diakonos (G1249) — "servant/minister/deacon"**: The word carries the semantic range of servant, minister, AND deacon. BibleHub's Helps: "properly, "thoroughly raise up dust by moving in a hurry, and so to minister"" — the root is about ACTION, about running errands, about serving. In the NT, diakonos usually refers to the Lord inspiring His servants to carry out His plan for His people. Jesus uses this word to say: the greatest among you must be one who SERVES — who carries out the Lord's plan, who ministers to others.
- **The article ὁ (the)**: The definite article before meizon means THE greatest — not "a great one" but THE greatest, the one who is most great. The article marks the superlative as DEFINITE: there IS a greatest, and that one shall be a servant.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- **Jesus models servanthood**: In John 13:14-15, Jesus washed his disciples' feet — performing the menial task of a servant to demonstrate that true greatness in God's kingdom is marked by humble service, not by elevated status. Matthew 23:11 extends this teaching: the greatest among the disciples must BE a servant, following Christ's own example.
- **The reversal of worldly greatness**: In the worldly order, the greatest person is served BY others. In God's kingdom, the greatest person SERVES others. Jesus reverses the worldly hierarchy: true greatness is not being served but serving. The one who is most capable has the most responsibility to serve — because God's kingdom operates on servanthood, not mastership.
- **Mark 10:45**: "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve." Jesus' own mission is servanthood. Matthew 23:11 extends this to the disciples: even the greatest among you must follow Christ's pattern — to serve, not to be served.
- **diakonos and "deacon"**: The English word "deacon" comes from this Greek root. A deacon in the early church was a servant — one who ministered to the poor, tended the widows, served the community. Jesus' use of diakonos means the greatest among the disciples must be a DEACON — a serving one, a ministering one. The word carries the full theological weight of the deacon's role: humble service, ministry, attending to others' needs.
