## 📖 Common English Translation (ESV)

> Neither be called instructors, for you have one instructor, the Christ.

---

## ✨ 4K Expanded Translation

*Original language nuances restored in readable English. Parenthetical notes explain the nuance recovered.*

Neither shall you be called instructors (a word that literally means "one who leads others down the road of learning" — a master-teacher who has walked the path and now guides others), since the one instructor of you is the Christ (and "Christ" literally means "the Anointed One" — not a name but an epithet: the one God has chosen and anointed, whose authority comes from divine anointing, not from earthly learning).

## 💡 Theological Insights Recovered

1. **"Instructor" = one who leads down the road of learning — Christ alone has walked the path**: kaθēgetai (G2519) from kata (down) + hēgeomai (to lead) literally means "one who leads others DOWN THE ROAD OF LEARNING." The Greek compound encodes the image of a guide who has walked the path and now leads others along it. Jesus' point: Christ alone can be the instructor because Christ alone has walked the path of divine knowledge — he alone has been anointed by God and sent.

2. **"Christ" = "the Anointed One" — divine authority, not earthly accomplishment**: Xristos (G5547) literally means "the Anointed One" — from χriω (to anoint with olive oil). Christ is not a name but an EPITHET: the one God has ANOINTED and sent. Jesus' authority as the one instructor comes not from his intelligence, his learning, or his charisma, but from DIVINE ANOINTING. God anointed him and gave him a mission. This is why Christ alone is the instructor: not because he is the most learned or the most capable, but because God CHOSE him and ANOINTED him.

**Grammatical Observations**:
- **μὴδe (G3366) — "neither/not even"**: The negative disjunctive accumulates the prohibitions. Not Rabbi (v8), not Father (v9), NEITHER instructor (v10). μὴδe = μὴ + δe = "not + moreover" = "not even." The force is: not even — go so far as to call yourselves instructors — NO. The accumulation builds a wall of prohibition: no title that elevates one above others.
- **kaθēgetai/kaθēgetes (G2519) — "instructor/leader/master"**: BibleHub's Helps Word-studies: "a leader; someone bringing others "down the road of learning" by giving needed instruction; a master-teacher." The compound from kata (down) + hēgeomai (to lead) literally means "one who leads down the path." KJV translates it "masters," ESV "instructors," NASB "leaders" — but the Greek carries ALL THREE nuances: one who has mastered the knowledge AND leads others AND instructs them in the way. Jesus forbids his disciples from claiming this title because Christ alone is the one who leads down the path.
- **ὅti (G3754) — "since/because" not "who"**: The Nestle 1904 text uses ὅti (since/because), not ὅstiς (whoever). This matters: the verse says "since the instructor of you is One, the Christ" — a causal explanation. Some manuscript variants use γar ("for") which is also explanatory. The causal ὅti makes the reason explicit: you cannot be called instructors BECAUSE there is one instructor, and He is Christ.
- **Xristos (G5547) — "the Anointed One"**: The word "Christ" comes from χriω (to anoint) — literally "the Anointed One." Thayer's lexicon: "the Sept. for מָשִׁיחַ (Messiah), anointed." Jesus is "the Christ" — the one God has anointed and sent. By naming himself as the one instructor, Jesus claims divine authority for his teaching: he alone instructs because he alone has been anointed by God.

**Cultural/Contextual Notes**:
- **Three titles, three prohibitions**: Verse 8 prohibits "Rabbi" (my great one), verse 9 prohibits "father," verse 10 prohibits "instructor/leader/master." The three titles represent three forms of earthly authority: the teacher's authority (Rabbi), the patriarchal authority (father), and the leadership authority (instructor). Jesus forbids all three because all authority belongs to Christ alone — the anointed one in heaven.
- **kaθēgetes and the road of learning**: BibleHub's Helps: "a leader; someone bringing others "down the road of learning" by giving needed instruction." The Greek compound literally encodes the image of LEADING SOMEONE DOWN A PATH. Jesus' point is that Christ alone can lead others down this path — because Christ alone has walked it. The disciples have not walked it; they are still learning. To call themselves "instructors" is to claim they have arrived when they are still en route.
- **The Christ as "the Anointed One"**: The word Xristos literally means "anointed" — from χriω, "to anoint with olive oil." Jesus is the one God has anointed (Genesis 4:30, Isaiah 53, Psalm 22). By identifying the one instructor as "the Christ," Jesus grounds his unique authority in divine anointing, not earthly accomplishment. Christ is the instructor not because he is smart or learned but because God has ANOINTED him.
